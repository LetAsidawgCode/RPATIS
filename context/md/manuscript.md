# CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

## Introduction

This study investigates the operational coordination and data management practices of the Municipal Assessor's Office and the Municipal Treasurer's Office, with specific focus on the challenges associated with manual record-keeping, legacy paper files, and disconnected departmental databases, and how a web-based Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization System can address these challenges. The study considers the need for an organized, transparent, and synchronized approach to managing real property tax valuation and revenue collection records.

This chapter introduces the project context and identifies the problem statement, the objectives of the study, purpose and description, scope and delimitations, theoretical and conceptual frameworks, and the definition of terms used in the study.

## Background of the Study

Local government units rely heavily on real property taxes to generate local revenues, which requires tight operational coordination between the Municipal Assessor's Office and the Municipal Treasurer’s Office. Globally, international frameworks and institutional studies emphasize that transparent land administration and digital integration are vital for effective public governance (United Nations, 2023). To support this, international evaluation by Schenker and Prichard (2024) highlights that subnational administrations frequently struggle with administrative disconnects and implementation complexities when managing property records.

In the Philippines, these administrative goals are governed by Republic Act No. 7160, which formally separates property appraisal tasks from independent tax collection duties. To strengthen this digital transition, the Department of Information and Communications Technology (2023) issued the Digital Government Masterplan to reduce manual paper across local government units. However, empirical studies examining local tax administration (Chinilla & Lagura, 2025) reveal that municipal offices continue to experience processing delays and communication gaps.

Municipal offices across Camarines Sur, covering Canaman, Magarao, San Fernando, and our primary benchmark site Pasacao, face heavy operational friction in property valuation and revenue collection. Even though our benchmark municipalities like Pasacao utilize localized software solutions like eRPT, operational bottlenecks still persist. Specifically, processing a Transfer of Assessment in Pasacao takes over seven days due to disconnected administrative steps. Conditions are even more challenging in heavily manual municipalities like Canaman, where assessing a new building requires days of preparation followed by an additional seven to fourteen days of waiting just to route paper-based approvals to the Provincial Capitol in Pili. As highlighted by Moksin et al. (2026), fragmented paper records and technological limitations account for a major share of performance variance in decentralized local tax administration.

These operational bottlenecks point directly to clear research gaps and technical bottlenecks. Municipal offices remain burdened by legacy paper records dating back to 1949, lack real-time document tracking monitors, and maintain completely separate databases between assessor rolls and treasury ledgers. As evaluated by Sunirah et al. (2026), standalone revenue platforms fail because they operate in isolation from core administrative workflows, forcing staff to manually cross-check records and causing prolonged delays. Furthermore, while systems like ETRACS automate core property valuations, they introduce heavy database migration overhead and lack built-in cross-office document routing.

To address these identified gaps and fulfill the purpose of this study, our group proposes a web-based Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization System. The platform utilizes a three-tier web architecture consisting of a frontend Presentation Tier for assessor evaluation screens and treasury validation views, a backend Application Tier for automated document routing and data integration logic, and a centralized Data Tier to store synchronized property files and transaction logs. By bridging municipal-treasury data silos and automating document routing, the project directly targets software quality standards, specifically focusing on functional suitability and performance efficiency to eliminate manual workflow delays in municipal assessor and treasurer offices.

## Objectives of the Study

### General Objective

The purpose of this study is to design, develop, and evaluate a web-based Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization System for municipal assessor and treasurer offices to bridge data silos and streamline workflow processing.

### Specific Objectives

Specifically, it aims to:

1. Assess the current manual processes, document routing delays, and operational challenges experienced by municipal assessor and treasurer offices across benchmark municipalities in Camarines Sur (Canaman, Magarao, San Fernando, and Pasacao).
2. Design and develop a three-tier web architecture featuring an assessor evaluation screen, a treasury validation view, an automated document routing engine, and a centralized database to synchronize property valuation records in real time.
3. Evaluate the developed system's adherence to ISO/IEC 25010 software quality standards, specifically focusing on functional suitability and performance efficiency.
4. Measure the effectiveness of the system in reducing turnaround time and eliminating manual reconciliation delays compared to existing practices.

## Scope and Delimitation

This study focuses on creating and testing a web-based Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization System to fix the communication gaps and data silos between municipal offices. The main goal is to look at current manual problems and build a three-tier web setup that includes screens for assessors and treasurers, automatic document routing, and real-time database updates.

The study involves municipal personnel, specifically staff and officials from the Municipal Assessor's Office and the Municipal Treasurer's Office, alongside IT evaluators who assess the system's technical quality. Geographically, the research is conducted in selected municipalities across Camarines Sur, specifically covering Canaman, Magarao, San Fernando, and our main benchmark site, Pasacao. The study, development, and evaluation are scheduled to run from July 2026 to November 2026. The main factors evaluated include transaction duration, document routing delays, database synchronization accuracy, and overall software performance. To achieve this, the study employs a descriptive-developmental design, incorporating needs assessment surveys, iterative system development, and ISO/IEC 25010 evaluations focused on functional suitability and performance efficiency.

For the delimitations, the system is strictly limited to tracking real property assessments and synchronizing records between the Municipal Assessor's Office and the Municipal Treasurer's Office. It does not cover other local government tasks like business permits, civil registry records, or general municipal accounting. Connecting with higher provincial offices, such as the Provincial Capitol in Pili, is limited only to tracking where documents are routed rather than rebuilding the entire provincial database. These boundaries are set to keep the project focused and manageable, making sure the software directly targets the main problem of disconnected records between the assessor and treasurer instead of expanding into unrelated areas.

Additionally, testing the system relies on controlled workflows and data from Pasacao and Canaman, meaning the results fit these specific types of municipalities and might not apply directly to large, highly urbanized cities. Finally, building the software is limited by the actual computer hardware available in lower-class local government units, which is why a lightweight web design is used instead of heavy, complex enterprise systems like ETRACS.

## Theoretical Framework

The theory that will be used in this study is the **Land Administration Domain Model (LADM) — Part 4: Valuation Information**, set by the International Organization for Standardization (2025). This standard provides clear ways to organize property values and transaction records so that land registries and tax collection offices can connect. A proper three-tier web setup and organized data rules will help fix delays, stop manual errors, and make communication smooth between the Municipal Assessor’s Office and the Municipal Treasurer’s Office; because a unified system keeps property and payment records matched in real time, it removes the need for manual cross-checking.

To support the system's ability to share data and connect offices, this study also uses the **Philippine e-Government Interoperability Framework (PeGIF)** from the Department of Information and Communications Technology (2023). This framework guides how local government units can set up standard data sharing and open communication rules. For this research, it helps explain how automating document routing between offices breaks down old data barriers and makes government services work the way they are supposed to.

# CHAPTER 2: REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES

This chapter presents the technical background, related literature, related studies, related systems, and a comprehensive synthesis and gap analysis that ground the development of the web-based Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization System.

## Technical Background

This section discusses the core technical foundations, system architectures, and data standards utilized in engineering the web-based Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization System. To eliminate the persistent communication barriers and data silos between the Municipal Assessor's Office and the Municipal Treasurer's Office, the system is designed around a three-tier web architecture. This architectural paradigm partitions the software into three distinct, loosely coupled layers: the Presentation Tier, the Application Tier, and the Data Tier.

The Presentation Tier delivers a responsive, browser-accessible user interface designed with specialized, role-tailored dashboards. This provides assessors with structured appraisal entry forms for Field Appraisal and Assessment Sheets (FAAS) and provides treasurers with real-time validation interfaces to monitor tax liabilities, collection ledgers, and payment clearances. Because it operates through standard web browsers, this tier eliminates the client-side installation overhead and dedicated hardware dependencies associated with legacy thick-client enterprise desktop suites.

The Application Tier serves as the central business logic controller, hosting the automated document routing engine, role-based access validation, and bi-directional transaction queues. This tier governs the sequential state machine of municipal assessment workflows, automatically dispatching property transactions across assessment encoding, appraisal approval, provincial endorsement, and treasury billing. Through event-driven asynchronous processing, the Application Tier ensures that updates made in assessment records trigger instantaneous recalculations in corresponding treasury tax ledgers.

The Data Tier implements a centralized relational database engineered with strict ACID (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability) transactional integrity. This layer maintains synchronized parcel inventories, Property Index Numbers (PIN), tax declaration registries, payment audit trails, and historical transaction logs. Relational constraints and foreign key linkages prevent orphan records, redundant data entry, and conflicting valuations between departments.

To guarantee structural data interoperability and long-term compliance with national and international land administration protocols, the schema design is anchored in the Land Administration Domain Model (LADM) Part 4: Valuation Information (ISO 19152-4:2025). This international standard establishes formal UML conceptual classes for parcel units, valuation registries, and taxation objects. Furthermore, the platform adheres to the Philippine e-Government Interoperability Framework (PeGIF) formulated by the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT, 2023), ensuring that data definitions, API endpoints, and payload formats follow national open-government data exchange standards. Combining a lightweight three-tier web deployment with ISO valuation conceptual models directly supports the real-time, low-overhead data synchronization required to replace manual paper handoffs in resource-constrained municipal environments.

## Review of Related Literature

Local real property taxation and municipal office coordination are shaped by a combination of legal mandates, national digital frameworks, and international system design principles. Examining these sources together reveals how legal requirements and technical gaps intersect in local government units like Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao and San Fernando.

### Legal Mandates and Administrative Compliance in Local Taxation

Property assessment and tax collection in Philippine municipalities are legally anchored in Republic Act No. 7160, or the Local Government Code of 1991 [Republic of the Philippines 1991]. As argued by the Anti-Red Tape Authority [2022] under Republic Act No. 11032, government offices must eliminate paper backlogs and adhere to strict processing windows. However, Section 248 of RA 7160 requires assessors to submit assessment rolls to treasurers only once a year, on or before December 31 [Republic of the Philippines 1991]. This creates severe administrative lag, leading to seven-day transfer delays in Pasacao and up to fourteen days of manual paperwork in Canaman.

In addition these administrative duties, recent legislation such as Republic Act No. 12001, the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA) [Republic of the Philippines 2024], compels local government units to adopt digital technologies and interface with the Bureau of Local Government Finance's Real Property Information System. While RA 12001 and ARTA Memorandum Circular No. 2022-02 [Anti-Red Tape Authority 2022] establish clear performance targets and modernization mandates, they function strictly as regulatory policies. They do not provide software code, database schemas, or automated document routing queues.

Furthermore, handling property records requires strict adherence to data privacy laws. Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012 [Republic of the Philippines 2012], and National Privacy Commission Circular No. 2022-04 [National Privacy Commission 2022] mandate technical and organizational safeguards to protect citizen information. In many municipal offices, legacy paper assessment rolls dating back to 1949 remain exposed on physical desks. While NPC Circular No. 2022-04 and RA 10173 establish compliance thresholds and penalties for data negligence, they do not prescribe encryption algorithms, role-based access control (RBAC) pipelines, or relational database structures for municipal tax workflows.

### National and International Digital Governance Frameworks

To address administrative fragmentation, national and international bodies provide overarching strategic frameworks. The Department of Information and Communications Technology [2023] outlined the Digital Government Masterplan (DGMP) 2023–2028 to dismantle departmental silos and digitize frontline public services. This is supported by the Philippine e-Government Interoperability Framework (PeGIF) [Department of Information and Communications Technology 2023], which sets technical and semantic standards for data exchange among local government units. Globally, the United Nations [2023] Sustainable Development Report (Targets 9.1 and 16.6) and the United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs [2024] E-Government Survey emphasize that unintegrated back-office databases severely hinder subnational governance.

To standardize property data internationally, the International Organization for Standardization [2025] introduced ISO 19152-4:2025, or Land Administration Domain Model (LADM) Part 4: Valuation Information. This standard establishes UML conceptual classes for parcel valuation and tax records. However, while DGMP, PeGIF, ISO 19152-4, and UN reports establish high-level architectural visions, semantic dictionaries, and global governance benchmarks, they operate at a macro scale. They deliberately omit physical relational DDL schemas, localized user interfaces, and automated inter-office document routing logic tailored to resource-constrained fourth-class municipalities.

### Real-World Problems with the Local Property Tax and IT Integration

Evaluating actual municipal implementation, international studies demonstrate that software investment frequently fails due to architectural isolation. Schenker and Prichard [2024], in their technical guidance for the Local Government Revenue Initiative (LoGRI), assert that monolithic software systems collapse in developing municipalities unless designed with modular subsystems and synchronized workflows. Similarly, Knebelmann [2022] reveals that standalone digital parcel registration projects often fail because disconnected back-office workflows prevent real-time data exchange between assessors and collection units.

Supporting these findings, Junquera-Varela and Lucas-Mas [2024], in the World Bank Revenue Administration Handbook, demonstrate that replacing manual ledger reconciliation with automated cross-system data matching eliminates revenue leakages and processing latencies. This is reinforced by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development [2024], which reports that advanced tax authorities achieve high efficiency through real-time digital registries and unified transactional queues. Finally, Okunogbe and Santoro [2023], in their systematic review published in The World Bank Research Observer, prove that digital tax interventions fail when implemented without synchronized back-office workflows. While these global studies establish the economic and operational necessity of IT integration, they provide broad policy frameworks rather than lightweight relational web architectures built specifically to resolve the paper-based friction between municipal assessor and treasurer offices in the Philippines.

## Review of Related Studies

The review of related studies examines the experimental research, regional surveys, and institutional evaluation conducted by prior researchers on municipal tax administration, property assessment workflows, and departmental information systems. Analyzing these studies provides empirical context for the operational bottlenecks experienced by local government units and highlights the technical limitations of existing software deployments.

### Local and Regional Assessment of Municipal Tax Administration

Recent studies on access Philippine municipalities show that even when computer systems are introduced, communication gaps and manual processing bottleneck still slow down coordination between municipal assessors and treasurers. In Davao del Norte, Chinilla and Lagura [2025] found through semi-structured interviews that while automated software can generate property declarations within minutes, actual assessment transfers still face two-to-three-day approval delays due to persistent communication barriers between departments.In Iloilo Province, Convocar et al. [2026] found that electronic collection software makes things more transparent, but it doesn't automatically match up the ledgers, so office staff still have to check over 90 percent of records by hand.

Looking at long-term records in La Trinidad, Benguet, Damong [2026] discovered that even when towns hit their collection goals, they still waste time tracking down unpaid taxes using paper records. In Camarines Sur, Reyes [2026], noted that local tax offices in Libmanan still struggle with slow processing times and communication barriers between departments. Lastly, Ternida and Azuelo [2025] found in Camarines Norte that checking tax declarations against cashbooks by hand creates backlogs that slow down the tax collection. These regional studies provide empirical evidence of the precise administrative frictions that the proposed system is engineered to resolve.

### Daily Inefficiencies, Record Fragmentation, and Workflow Bottlenecks

Other researchers have also looked at how broken records and a lack of data sharing slow down property assessment. Fuentes and Salapa [2023], showed that when people can’t visit physical counters easily, unpaid taxes go up. In Sulu Province, Hassan-Tahil and Asiri [2024] found that municipal workers really want computerized assessment tools because it stops them from making duplicate records.

Moksin et al. [2026] found in Malaysia that missing property records and zero data sharing between offices completely paralyze assessment work. Senawi and Osmadi [2024] also found in West Malaysia that old software and scattered records cause major delays, leaving property values unrevised for up to 35 years. Finally, Sunirah et al. [2026] discovered a problem in Indonesia, even when online registrations increased, total tax money collected actually went down because the back-office ledgers were never updated together. These studies prove that front-end digital tools don't work if the back-office records remain separated.

### Database Architecture, IT Integration, and Revenue Impact Metrics

Other studies focused on why database setup and IT integrations matter for good tax collection. Chaputula and Chingamtolo [2026] showed that while organizing records helps property registration, poor handoffs between assessment and revenue offices still slow while down billings. In Ghana, Dzagah et al. [2025] proved that keeping clear and accountable records builds citizen trust and helps tax compliance, while messy paper records lead to more unpaid taxes.

In Rwanda, Mungwarakarama and Muhayimana [2025] found that office workflows account for most of a town's revenue success, especially payment processing and parcel registration. Wadesango et al. [2024] also proved in Zimbabwe that good IT systems are responsible for over 80% of tax administration efficiency, noting that lacking a central property database is the main problem. Lastly, Wangai [2023] found that using ICT helps speed up tax payments in Rwanda. These studies show why our proposed system needs a centralized database that connects offices directly.

## Review of Related Systems

This presents the significant related systems that the researchers have examined to expand their knowledge about the study and to comprehend the research topic. ### Enterprise and National Municipal Revenue Systems

Existing municipal revenue platforms show that while computerizing appraisal records helps standardize property valuation, disconnected office software structures still create serious operational gaps. In the system developed by Rameses Systems Inc. [2022], the Enhanced Tax Revenue and Collection System (ETRACS) uses an enterprise framework to connect Field Appraisal and Assessment Sheet encoding, tax declaration generation, and treasury tax collection workflows. It was revealed in Rameses Systems Inc [2022] research that ETRACS validates the core model of connecting the assessor records directly to treasure ledger to prevent billing errors. However, ETRACS operates an installed enterprise suite requiring complex local server infrastructure and dedicated workstation, which creates heavy maintenance barriers for small local governments. Furthermore, it contains no workflow tracker to monitor physical paper movement or provincial approvals, and migrating old paper records into it without a localized needs assessment often causes severe backlogs.

Similarly, it was stated by the Bureau of Local Government Finance [2022], that the Electronic Real Property Tax System (LGU-eRPTS) provides local units with modules to compute appraisal values and generate assessment rolls. As the main baseline evaluated across Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao, and San Fernando, LGU-eRPTS proves that computerizing appraisals creates necessary valuation consistency. However, field observations in Pasacao reveal major structural flaws. The software runs as an isolated departmental database installation where assessment updates do not link dynamically to treasury ledger. Processing a property transfer still takes over seven days because staff rely on physical paper endorsement and hand-carried clearance slips. These studies discoveries will prove particularly valuable, educating on the exact architectural limits that our proposed web-based synchronization platform and routing modules resolve without heavy enterprise overhead.

### Metropolitan and Local Online Tax Portals

Other local government units have launched online portals to let property owners view tax balances and pay online. According to the City Government of Muntinlupa [2023], the Muntinlupa Online Real Property Assessment and Payment System (MORPS) allows registered owners to query tax balances and pay through electronic gateways. It was revealed in their system documentation that MORPS helps reduce counter congestion using Property Index Numbers, but it functions strictly as a treasury- managed payment tool that stays completely isolated from the Assessor’s internal appraisal workflow. When owners need to process property transfers or correct valuation errors, the portal offers no tracking tools, forcing taxpayers to visit the assessor’s office in person.

In the same way, it was shown in the study of the City Government of Iloilo[2023] that their online portal enables owners to generate digital tax orders of payment and settle liabilities electronically. While it reduces office queues, it remains an isolated billing inquiry tool relying on pre-compiled assessment tools with no data synchronization or document tracking. Similar limitations appear in the Makatizen Portal [City Government of Makati 2022], the Baguio In-My-Pocket Portal [City Government of Baguio 2022], and the Antipolo Online Portal [City Government of Antipolo 2022], all of which allow remote payment viewing but lock out electronic processing when parcels involve delinquent accounts, boundary adjustments, or active property transfers, requiring physical paper routing instead. These studies' discoveries will prove particularly valuable, highlighting why front-end payment portals fail if internal inter-office assessment workflows remain disconnected.

### International Municipal and Centralized Valuation Platforms

Looking at international platforms, centralized digital portals offer different models for property data management. It was shown in the research of the Municipal Property Assessment Corporation [2022]in Ontario, Canada, that AboutMyProperty gives owners digital access to valuation notices and classification records. However, MPAC operates on a decoupled annual batch-delivery model, transmitting rolls to local towns only once a year without workflow tools for daily mid-cycle transactions or building permits.

Furthermore, it was stated by the Cook County Assessor's Office [2022] in Illinois that their online portal allows querying assessment values and filing appeals using Property Index Numbers. However, it stays decoupled from the county treasurer's tax collection database, requiring external batch reconciliations that cause coordination lags. Similar structural gaps are documented in the NSW Valuation and Land Values Portal [NSW Valuer General 2023], HM Land Registry Service [HM Land Registry 2022], the Philadelphia Property Assessment Portal [City Government of Philadelphia 2022], SAILIS in South Australia [Government of South Australia 2022], e-LAVS in Jamaica [National Land Agency of Jamaica 2022], and the Cape Town Valuation Portal [City of Cape Town 2023] where national registries or periodic rolls operate on fixed schedules and lack real-time document tracking for local departmental clearances. These studies' discoveries will prove particularly valuable, shedding light on how our proposed system adapts centralized concepts into a localized, real-time synchronization pipeline for Philippine municipal offices.

## Synthesis and Gap Analysis

Looking across all the literature, empirical studies, and existing software systems reviewed in this chapter, a clear pattern emerges that connects broad government policies down to local office needs. On a broad scale, global and national policies such as the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, the DICT Digital Government Masterplan, and ARTA regulations push for transparent, paperless, and digital public services. Moving closer down, legal frameworks like the Local Government Code (RA 7160), RPVARA (RA 12001), and the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173), alongside technical guides like PeGIF and ISO 19152-4, set the required standards for data sharing, secure processing, and property valuation.

However, despite these existing policies and standards, a major research gap remains. Prior empirical studies prove that computerizing front-end collections or deploying standalone software does not solve administrative lag if back-office ledgers remain disconnected. Furthermore, evaluating existing systems shows that heavy enterprise suites like ETRACS introduce massive deployment and maintenance overhead for fourth-class municipalities, while localized eRPT platforms and online payment portals operate in isolated silos without document tracking or cross-office ledger synchronization.

In municipalities across Camarines Sur specifically in benchmark sites like Pasacao and Canaman local offices remain burdened by legacy paper records dating back to 1949 and suffer from severe processing delays, such as property transfers taking over seven days due to manual paper endorsements and physical trips to the Provincial Capitol in Pili. Existing tools lack automated routing queues and real-time database links between assessors and treasurers.

To bridge this exact gap, our study proposes the web-based Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization System using a three-tier web architecture. By translating national mandates, ISO valuation classes, and data privacy rules into automated document routing queues and real-time database synchronization, our system directly removes the manual workflow bottlenecks between the Municipal Assessor's Office and the Municipal Treasurer's Office.
