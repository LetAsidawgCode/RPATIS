# Property Tax Reassessment Among Local Authorities: The Implementation and Its Key Challenges

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Azhari Senawi and Atasya Osmadi. 2024. Property tax reassessment among local authorities: the implementation and its key challenges. Journal of Financial Management of Property and Construction 29, 1 (2024), 152–167. https://doi.org/10.1108/JFMPC-04-2022-0022**

- **Summary:** Empirical findings from partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) across West Malaysian local councils by Senawi and Osmadi (2024) confirmed that obsolete property software and fragmented departmental archives generate severe administrative inertia, causing municipal valuation rolls to remain unrevised for up to thirty-five years.
 
- **Relevance:** This empirical investigation directly justifies the operational necessity and baseline parameters evaluated in the proposed system's Needs Assessment & Comparative Evaluation Module. Senawi and Osmadi’s structural model confirms that software obsolescence drives assessment backlogs, justifying the event-triggered state monitors, automated routing queues, and relational table mappings engineered into the Assessment Request Routing Module. Legacy records paralyze municipal reassessment. In Camarines Sur municipalities such as Canaman, valuation rolls and physical property cards dating back to 1949 create massive administrative friction during reassessment and ownership transfers. While Senawi and Osmadi provide robust econometric evidence of valuation stagnation, their research ends at statistical diagnosis; they offer no functional software tools, relational database designs, or real-time document tracking interfaces. The proposed system directly resolves this operational failure by developing an integrated web platform that digitizes property assessment workflows, automates inter-departmental routing, and synchronizes updated valuation rolls directly with local treasury payment records.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 4: Reassessment Implementation and Challenges (pp. 158–161):_ Reports empirical survey findings revealing that municipal councils operate with outdated valuation rolls for up to 35 years due to software deficiencies and fragmented records.
  - _Section 5: Conclusion and Practical Implications (pp. 163–165):_ Recommends that local government authorities replace manual file cross-referencing with automated, integrated property tracking tools to eliminate administrative valuation bottlenecks.
