# Financial Accountability, Trust, and Property Tax Compliance: Experiences from Sub-Saharan Africa

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Kingsley Kwasi Dzagah, Redeemer Krah, and Lexis Alexander Tetteh. 2025. Financial Accountability, Trust, and Property Tax Compliance: Experiences from Sub-Saharan Africa. International Journal of Public Administration (March 2025). https://doi.org/10.1080/01900692.2025.2443026**

- **Summary:** Applying structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) to survey data from municipal assemblies in Ghana, Dzagah et al. (2025) demonstrated that municipal financial accountability directly dictates citizen trust and property tax compliance ($\text{Accountability} \rightarrow \text{Trust} \rightarrow \text{Compliance}$), warning that opaque manual record-keeping actively fosters delinquency.

- **Relevance:** This empirical study provides the behavioral and institutional justification for the Administrative Workflow Panel and tracking monitors within the proposed system's comparative evaluation across Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao, and San Fernando. Grounded in Dzagah et al.’s accountability-trust structural pathway, the proposed system directly informs the browser-based tracking monitors and real-time status queues providing transparent visibility across assessment reviews and payment verification stages. Opaque municipal processes breed public suspicion. When property owners cannot track where their assessment folders sit in municipal corridors, administrative delays fuel frustration and disincentivize timely tax settlement. Although Dzagah et al. prove the econometric link between record transparency and compliance, their research operates strictly as a behavioral survey; they construct no software platforms, transaction status queues, or synchronized databases. The proposed system directly resolves this transparency gap by delivering a web-based document tracking monitor that provides end-to-end visibility into assessment approvals and synchronizes tax records across assessor and treasurer departments in real time.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Theoretical Framework & Hypotheses (pp. 3–6):_ Uses Stewardship Theory to formulate the structural model linking municipal financial accountability, institutional trust, and property rate compliance.
  - _Results & Structural Model Evaluation (pp. 8–12):_ Validates via PLS-SEM that record transparency significantly increases tax compliance, while administrative opacity in paper-bound departments triggers substantial revenue delinquency.
