# Evaluating the Effectiveness of a Land and Building Tax Information System in Improving Property Tax Collection: Evidence from Madiun City, Indonesia

**Category:** [[Related Study international C2]]

**Iroh Sunirah, Kridawati Sadhana, Roos Widjajani, and Ratih Nindita Kirana. 2026. Evaluating the Effectiveness of a Land and Building Tax Information System in Improving Property Tax Collection: Evidence from Madiun City, Indonesia. International Journal of Management and Economics Invention 12, 7 (July 2026), 5844–5859. https://doi.org/10.47191/ijmei/v12i7.09**

- **Summary:** Investigating the municipal Land and Building Tax Service Information System (SIP-PBBKU) in Madiun City, Indonesia, Sunirah et al. (2026) documented a critical administrative paradox where digital taxpayer registrations increased from 43,977 to 44,987 between 2023 and 2024 while realized property tax revenue contracted from IDR 23.88 billion to IDR 22.99 billion due to disconnected back-office ledger reconciliation.

- **Relevance:** This empirical investigation validates the core architectural premise of the proposed system's comparative evaluation across Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao, and San Fernando. Demonstrating that public-facing portals fail when back-office registries remain disconnected, Sunirah et al.’s findings directly inform the automated foreign-key mapping, asynchronous reconciliation routines, and dual-office database schemas engineered into the Tax Payment Synchronization Module. Front-end digitization alone solves nothing. Even in Pasacao, where a localized electronic property tax system is actively utilized, property transfers still take over seven days because back-office assessment updates require manual cross-checking against treasury receipts. While Sunirah et al. thoroughly diagnose this administrative breakdown in Madiun City, their study remains a public administration evaluation; they offer no relational database schemas, automated tracking state machines, or web middleware code to resolve inter-departmental divides. The proposed system directly overcomes this operational bottleneck by deploying an integrated web platform that synchronizes municipal assessor valuation records with treasury payment ledgers in real time, eliminating delayed manual reconciliations.

- **Source Reference(s):**

  - _Results & Assessment Matrix (pp. 5850–5854):_ Identifies that high satisfaction with citizen-facing digital interfaces was undermined by severe back-office delays in manual receivables reconciliation between village collectors and central revenue officers.
