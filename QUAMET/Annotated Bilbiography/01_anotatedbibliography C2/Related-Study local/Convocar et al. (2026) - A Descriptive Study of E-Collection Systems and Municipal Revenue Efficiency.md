# A Descriptive Study of E-Collection Systems and Municipal Revenue Efficiency: Evidence from Selected Municipalities in Iloilo Province, Philippines

**Category:** [[Related Study local C2]]

**Blessed Art Marie L. Convocar, Marjo F. Feguro, Rachylle Claryce T. Querimit, Irish Faith A. Solinap, Bernandino P. Malang, and Jocelyn DS. Malang. 2026. A Descriptive Study of E-Collection Systems and Municipal Revenue Efficiency: Evidence from Selected Municipalities in Iloilo Province, Philippines. International Journal of Multidisciplinary: Applied Business and Education Research 7, 2 (February 2026), 770–791. https://doi.org/10.11594/ijmaber.07.02.22**

- **Summary:** A descriptive survey of municipal treasury and accounting personnel across Iloilo Province by Convocar et al. (2026) determined that while electronic collection software elevates transaction transparency (mean = 3.96) and payment timeliness (mean = 3.74), standalone deployments fail to automate ledger reconciliation, leaving over 90% of municipal records bound to manual clerical verification.
 
- **Relevance:** This empirical study provides localized Philippine evidence validating the technical necessity of the Tax Payment Synchronization Module within the proposed system's comparative evaluation across Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao, and San Fernando. Demonstrating that unintegrated revenue tools leave over 90% of transactions dependent on manual verification, Convocar et al.’s metrics directly justify the automated foreign-key joins, event-driven ledger reconciliation routines, and centralized relational table mappings engineered into the Tax Payment Synchronization Module. Standalone software preserves administrative silos. In Pasacao, the existing localized eRPT platform operates in isolation from the Assessor’s back office, leaving property transfers stalled for over seven days while staff manually verify tax clearances. Although Convocar et al. deliver robust descriptive survey metrics across municipal finance offices, their investigation is limited to evaluating existing commercial systems; they offer no architectural designs, relational foreign-key schemas, or document tracking state machines. The proposed system resolves this operational limitation by deploying an integrated web platform that bridges municipal assessor valuation cards directly with treasury collection ledgers in real time, eliminating manual cross-checking bottlenecks.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Abstract & Section 4: Results (pp. 770, 778–782):_ Reports high weighted means for transaction transparency ($WM = 3.96$) and processing timeliness ($WM = 3.74$), contrasted against lower scores for real-time ledger reconciliation and delinquency tracking.
  - _Section 5: Discussion & Institutional Barriers (pp. 784–787):_ Concludes that deploying revenue collection tools without inter-departmental integration forces local governments to process over 90% of administrative records manually.
