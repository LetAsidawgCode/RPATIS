# Assessing Local Revenue Code Implementation: Perceptions of Micro-Entrepreneurs in Libmanan, Camarines Sur

**Category:** [[Related Study local C2]]

**Patrick John C. Reyes. 2026. Assessing Local Revenue Code Implementation: Perceptions of Micro-Entrepreneurs in Libmanan, Camarines Sur. Aloysian Interdisciplinary Journal of Social Sciences, Education, and Allied Fields 2, 5 (2026). https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20428663**

- **Summary:** Grounded in survey responses from 304 micro-entrepreneurs in Libmanan, Camarines Sur, Reyes (2026) demonstrated that despite the deployment of computerized revenue tools, municipal tax administration remains crippled by procedural complexity, inter-departmental communication divides, and extensive transaction turnaround delays.
 
- **Relevance:** This empirical study provides immediate localized geographic evidence from Camarines Sur directly grounding the proposed system's comparative evaluation across Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao, and San Fernando. Conducted in an adjacent first-class municipality, Reyes’s findings directly guide the browser-based tracking monitors and inter-office routing workflows engineered into the Assessment Request Routing and Document Tracking Module. Provincial proximity highlights shared bottlenecks. Across Camarines Sur, local businesses experience extensive waiting periods because municipal assessment approvals and treasury payment clearances operate as disconnected bureaucratic islands. While Reyes rigorously documents taxpayer frustration with procedural complexity and communication barriers, his research focuses strictly on perceptual evaluation; he provides no software designs, document routing state machines, or relational database pipelines. The proposed system directly resolves this localized operational divide by deploying an integrated web platform that provides real-time document tracking and bridges municipal assessor evaluations directly with treasury collection windows.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Abstract & Section 3: Empirical Findings (pp. 1–3):_ Surveys 304 business owners in Libmanan, Camarines Sur, confirming that municipal revenue administration suffers from severe procedural bottlenecks and document turnaround latencies.
  - _Section 4: Discussion & Recommendations (pp. 4–6):_ Identifies inter-departmental communication gaps between municipal assessment and treasury collection personnel as the primary friction point impeding local public transaction throughput.
