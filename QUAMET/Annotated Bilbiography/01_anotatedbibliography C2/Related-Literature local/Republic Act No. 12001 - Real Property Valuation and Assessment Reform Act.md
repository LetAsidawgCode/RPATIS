# Republic Act No. 12001: Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA)

**Category:** [[Related Literature local C2]]

**Republic of the Philippines. 2024. Republic Act No. 12001: An Act Instituting Reforms in Real Property Valuation and Assessment in the Philippines, Reorganizing the Bureau of Local Government Finance, Granting Tax Amnesty on Real Property and Special Levies on Real Property, and Appropriating Funds Therefor (Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act). Official Gazette of the Republic of the Philippines, Manila. https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/2024/06/13/republic-act-no-12001/**

- **Summary:** Signed into law on June 13, 2024, Republic Act No. 12001 establishes the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (RPVARA), mandating local government units to adopt digital technology and interface with the Bureau of Local Government Finance’s Real Property Information System (RPIS) to maintain an electronic database of property transactions.

- **Relevance:** This recent statutory mandate provides the most immediate legislative justification for the proposed system's comparative evaluation across Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao, and San Fernando. Enforcing Section 3(e) and 3(h), the law compels municipal assessors to maintain digitized assessment rolls and adopt digital platforms. Within the proposed system, these national mandates directly inform the design of event-triggered state monitors, automated assessment routing queues, and relational table mappings connecting the Assessment Request Routing Module with the Tax Payment Synchronization Module. Modernization mandates frequently outpace municipal capacities. While RPVARA establishes national valuation schedules and envisions a centralized RPIS database, it leaves local inter-office coordination completely unspecified. Fourth-class municipalities burdened by manual paper records dating back to 1949 receive no tangible software modules, tracking queues, or relational reconciliation schemas to connect assessor valuation changes with treasury tax collection windows. The proposed system directly resolves this operational disconnect by engineering an integrated web platform that automates document handoffs, tracks assessment statuses in real time, and synchronizes property values directly with local tax collection ledgers.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 3(e) & 3(h): Declaration of Policy and Objectives (pp. 1–2):_ Mandates the establishment of an up-to-date electronic database of all real property transactions and promotes digital technology in local property tax administration to strengthen municipal revenue generation.
  - _Section 5(g) & 5(i): Powers and Functions of the BLGF (pp. 2–3):_ Authorizes the Bureau of Local Government Finance to maintain the central Real Property Information System (RPIS) and enforce uniform administrative workflows for property transfers, annotations, and tax declaration issuances across local assessor offices.
