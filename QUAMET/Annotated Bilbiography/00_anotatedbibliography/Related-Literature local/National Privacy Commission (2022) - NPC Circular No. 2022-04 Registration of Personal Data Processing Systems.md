# NPC Circular No. 2022-04: Registration of Personal Data Processing Systems and Governance Standards

**Category:** [[Related Literature local]]

**National Privacy Commission. 2022. NPC Circular No. 2022-04: Registration of Personal Data Processing Systems, Notification Regarding Automated Decision-Making or Profiling. Republic of the Philippines, Pasay City. https://privacy.gov.ph/memorandum-circulars/**

- **Summary:** The National Privacy Commission (2022) promulgated Circular No. 2022-04, establishing mandatory registration, technical security benchmarks, and governance standards for personal data processing systems in government instrumentalities, mandating that local government units maintain risk assessments, access controls, and technical safeguards when digitizing sensitive citizen property and tax databases.

- **Relevance:** This regulatory issuance defines the data privacy and security compliance criteria required for the proposed system's feasibility evaluation, specifically governing the protection of property ownership ledgers, taxpayer identification files, and real estate valuations. While the NPC legally mandates data protection controls for all government data processing systems, municipal assessor and treasurer offices in smaller municipalities routinely operate unmanaged paper archives or unprotected spreadsheets that expose sensitive citizen data. The proposed system addresses this regulatory standard by conducting a local needs assessment to document current data security practices in benchmark LGUs and evaluating the technical feasibility of incorporating role-based access control and database encryption within the proposed synchronization architecture.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 5: Mandatory Registration of Government Data Processing Systems (pp. 3–5):_ Classifies municipal government administrative databases as critical data processing systems requiring formal registration, security oversight, and appointed data protection officers.
  - _Section 9: Technical Security and Access Control Measures (pp. 7–9):_ Mandates automated transaction logging, strict role-based user authentication, and data minimization protocols to prevent unauthorized viewing or tampering with public records.
