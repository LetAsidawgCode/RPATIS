# Key Elements of IT System Design for Property Taxation

**Category:** [[Related Literature international]]

**Xaver Schenker and Wilson Prichard. 2024. Key Elements of IT System Design for Property Taxation. LoGRI Guidance Note No. 04. Local Government Revenue Initiative, University of Toronto, Toronto, ON, Canada. https://logri.org/resources/key-elements-of-it-system-design-for-property-taxation/**

- **Summary:** Schenker and Prichard (2024) formulated an international architectural guidance framework for property tax digitalization in resource-constrained subnational administrations, identifying key functional requirements across property identification, valuation, billing, collection, and reporting while demonstrating that rigid commercial off-the-shelf software and siloed database architectures frequently collapse due to high licensing costs and lack of local administrative interoperability.

- **Relevance:** This technical guidance directly justifies the operational need for developing the proposed system by establishing modularity, inter-departmental data exchange, and local configurability as prerequisite feasibility criteria for municipal property tax administration. While Schenker and Prichard synthesize global best practices, their guidance lacks an empirical needs assessment of municipal workflow delays in Philippine local government contexts. The proposed system addresses this gap by conducting a local needs assessment across benchmark municipalities in Camarines Sur to establish the operational necessity and architectural specifications for a web-based tracking and synchronization system bridging municipal assessor and treasurer workflows.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Core Administrative Functions (pp. 4–8):_ Delineates the sequential functional pipeline connecting property discovery and valuation to rate calculation, billing, collection, and compliance monitoring.
  - _System Design Principles (pp. 9–12):_ Highlights modular architecture, local configurability, and database interoperability as essential safeguards against vendor lock-in and departmental data fragmentation.
