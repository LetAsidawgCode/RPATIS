# The Promise and Limitations of Information Technology for Tax Mobilization

**Category:** [[Related Literature international]]

**Oyebola Okunogbe and Fabrizio Santoro. 2023. The Promise and Limitations of Information Technology for Tax Mobilization. The World Bank Research Observer 38, 2 (August 2023), 295–324. https://doi.org/10.1093/wbro/lkac008**

- **Summary:** Okunogbe and Santoro (2023) conducted a systematic review of the empirical literature on information technology adoption in tax administration across low- and middle-income countries, establishing that digital tools improve tax base identification, compliance monitoring, and administrative facilitation but consistently fail when deployed without institutional mainstreaming, adequate infrastructure, and regulatory support for cross-agency data sharing.

- **Relevance:** This international policy review directly justifies the institutional rationale and technical feasibility framing of the proposed system's needs assessment module by demonstrating that IT adoption for tax administration in resource-constrained municipalities achieves measurable efficiency gains only when assessment and collection workflows are digitally integrated rather than operated as isolated departmental subsystems. While Okunogbe and Santoro synthesize macro-level evidence across national tax authorities, their review does not examine localized inter-office synchronization architectures between subnational assessor and treasurer offices operating under fragmented physical record systems. The proposed system operationalizes this review's core finding by developing an integrated web platform that embeds digital tracking and payment synchronization directly into the Municipal Assessor's and Treasurer's back-office workflows, addressing the institutional mainstreaming gap documented across developing-country municipal administrations.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 3: IT for Tax Administration — Monitoring Compliance (pp. 302–307):_ Demonstrates that automated data checks and electronic registry integration between assessing and collecting agencies are the most impactful IT interventions for reducing revenue leakage and reconciliation discrepancies.
  - _Section 4: Limitations — Institutional Mainstreaming (pp. 313–316):_ Identifies the absence of inter-agency data sharing protocols and inadequate regulatory frameworks as primary barriers that prevent IT investments from delivering efficiency gains in subnational tax administration.
