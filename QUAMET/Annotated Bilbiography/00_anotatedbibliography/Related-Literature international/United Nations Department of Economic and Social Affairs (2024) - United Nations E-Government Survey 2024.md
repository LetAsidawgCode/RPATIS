# United Nations E-Government Survey 2024: Accelerating Digital Transformation for Sustainable Development

**Category:** [[Related Literature international]]

**United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs. 2024. United Nations E-Government Survey 2024: Accelerating Digital Transformation for Sustainable Development. United Nations, New York, NY. https://desapublications.un.org/publications/united-nations-e-government-survey-2024**

- **Summary:** The United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs (2024) assessed global digital governance across 193 member states and expanded the Local Online Services Index (LOSI) to 95 indicators across six dimensions, establishing that persistent departmental data silos, uncoordinated municipal software subsystems, and fragmented back-office records severely restrict local public service delivery compared to national digital progress.

- **Relevance:** This global e-governance assessment directly justifies the operational necessity of the proposed system's needs assessment module by providing a recognized taxonomic framework to evaluate administrative data silos at the municipal level. While UNDESA measures municipal e-service disparities through broad international indicators, it does not assess localized transaction latency or daily cross-checking friction between municipal assessor and treasurer offices. The proposed system addresses this gap by conducting a localized needs assessment across benchmark municipalities in Camarines Sur to determine operational frictions and evaluate the technical feasibility of developing a web-based inter-office synchronization system.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Chapter 3: Assessment of Local E-Government and the Local Online Services Index (pp. 95–118):_ Expands the LOSI framework to 95 indicators, documenting that subnational institutions lag significantly behind national portals due to fragmented local IT infrastructure.
  - _Digital Government Model Framework (pp. 145–160):_ Identifies inter-agency data interoperability, institutional data governance, and departmental integration as critical prerequisites for effective municipal public administration.
