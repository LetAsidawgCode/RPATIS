# Digitalisation of Property Taxation in Developing Countries: Recent Advances and Remaining Challenges

**Category:** [[Related Literature international]]

**Justine Knebelmann. 2022. Digitalisation of Property Taxation in Developing Countries: Recent Advances and Remaining Challenges. Centre for Tax Analysis in Developing Countries (TaxDev) / Overseas Development Institute, London, UK. https://odi.org/en/publications/digitalisation-of-property-taxation-in-developing-countries-recent-advances-and-remaining-challenges/**

- **Summary:** Knebelmann (2022) conducted a comparative analysis of more than 15 municipal property tax digitalization initiatives across developing economies, finding that deploying digital tools to construct comprehensive fiscal property registers substantially increases coverage, but administrative disconnects and implementation complexities frequently impede revenue synchronization.

- **Relevance:** This international policy report directly justifies the problem domain and urgency of the proposed system by demonstrating that standalone digital investments fail to achieve operational efficiency when back-office administrative workflows remain disconnected. While Knebelmann synthesizes the benefits of digitized cadastres and property identification tools across developing nations, the report focuses on macro-level policy lessons rather than the engineering of inter-office document tracking platforms that eliminate physical paper handoffs between assessing and revenue-collecting offices. The proposed system resolves this bottleneck by implementing an integrated tracking and synchronization web architecture that links valuation evaluations directly to treasury verification workflows.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 2: Overview of Digital Innovations in Property Taxation (pp. 8–15):_ Documents empirical gains in property discovery and tax roll expansion achieved through digital registers and GIS parcel mapping across municipal pilots.
  - _Section 4: Implementation Bottlenecks and Cross-Agency Coordination (pp. 24–30):_ Identifies persistent administrative latency and departmental fragmentation between assessment authorities and revenue collectors as major barriers to tax administration efficiency.
