# Enhanced Tax Revenue Assessment and Collection System Through the Lens of the Municipal Treasurers and Assessors

**Category:** [[Related Study local]]

**[Norberto Arrocena Chinilla and Glenne Berja Lagura. 2025. Enhanced Tax Revenue Assessment and Collection System Through the Lens of the Municipal Treasurers and Assessors. International Journal for Multidisciplinary Research (IJFMR) 7, 4 (July–August 2025), 1–42. DOI:https://doi.org/10.36948/ijfmr.2025.v07i04.51726]**

- **Summary:** Chinilla and Lagura investigate the operational implementation of the Enhanced Tax Revenue Assessment and Collection System across eight municipalities in Davao del Norte through key informant interviews with fifteen municipal assessors and treasurers, revealing that while computerized taxation modules expedite tax declaration generation to minutes, assessment transfers still encounter two-to-three-day approval delays and suffer from data discrepancies arising from persistent coordination gaps between assessor and treasurer departments.

- **Relevance:** This study provides empirical evidence that operational disconnects between municipal assessor and treasurer offices cause ledger inconsistencies, reconciliation errors, and transaction delays. While ETRACS automates core valuation, it suffers from heavy ledger migration overhead and lacks cross-office document tracking. The proposed system resolves these limitations through a lightweight web architecture providing real-time document routing and payment synchronization without cumbersome software overhead.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _[Chapter IV, Major Themes, pp. 14–16]:_ Documents that routine tax declaration issuance operates within minutes while property ownership transfers require two to three days, emphasizing that communication gaps between the Municipal Assessor’s Office (MASSO) and Municipal Treasurer’s Office (MTO) produce record inconsistencies and workflow friction.
  - _[Chapter IV, System Limitations, pp. 23–25]:_ Details that data migration from manual ledgers to digital platforms required building over 9,000 property ledgers over a two-year period, resulting in ledger compatibility errors, tax computation discrepancies, and operational downtime.
