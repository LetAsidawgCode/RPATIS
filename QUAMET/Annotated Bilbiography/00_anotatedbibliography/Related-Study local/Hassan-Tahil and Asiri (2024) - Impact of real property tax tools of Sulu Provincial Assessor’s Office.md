# Impact of real property tax tools of Sulu Provincial Assessor’s Office: employees’ perspectives

**Category:** [[Related Study local]]

**[Normalynne J. Hassan-Tahil and Masnona S. Asiri. 2024. Impact of real property tax tools of Sulu Provincial Assessor’s Office: employees’ perspectives. Social Psychology and Human Experience 1, 1 (April 2024), 1–14. DOI:https://doi.org/10.62596/8newjy87]**

- **Summary:** Hassan-Tahil and Asiri evaluate employee perceptions of property tax tools across nineteen municipalities in Sulu through a quantitative survey of one hundred local personnel, revealing strong agreement on the effectiveness of tax mapping (mean = 4.61) and electronic computerization (mean = 4.53) in curbing duplicate property records and manual assessment errors.

- **Relevance:** This study demonstrates that municipal personnel strongly favor computerized assessment tools to eliminate duplicate property records and manual calculation errors. However, the authors focus solely on internal valuation tools without addressing inter-departmental workflow friction or document routing to the treasury. The proposed system resolves this operational boundary by connecting computerized assessment tracking directly to treasurer payment validation through real-time data synchronization.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _[Section 2 Operational Background & Section 5 Results, pp. 2, 6–7]:_ Identifies pre-existing manual issues including duplicate property records and unmapped parcels, while quantitative survey results establish high employee ratings for tax mapping (mean = 4.61, SD = 0.393) and computerization (mean = 4.53, SD = 0.401).
  - _[Section 5 Correlation Analysis, Table 3, p. 10]:_ Reports a significant positive correlation between electronic computerization and schedule of market values (Pearson r = 0.641, p < 0.001), indicating strong interdependency between digital systems and valuation accuracy.
