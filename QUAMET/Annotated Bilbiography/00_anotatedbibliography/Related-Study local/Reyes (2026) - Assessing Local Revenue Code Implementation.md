# Assessing Local Revenue Code Implementation: Perceptions of Micro-Entrepreneurs in Libmanan, Camarines Sur

**Category:** [[Related Study local]]

**[Patrick John C. Reyes. 2026. Assessing Local Revenue Code Implementation: Perceptions of Micro-Entrepreneurs in Libmanan, Camarines Sur. Aloysian Interdisciplinary Journal of Social Sciences, Education, and Allied Fields 2, 5 (2026). DOI:https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20428663]**

- **Summary:** Reyes evaluates the operational implementation of the Local Revenue Code across 304 micro-entrepreneurs in Libmanan, Camarines Sur, revealing that while administrative personnel and digital tax tools like ETRACS are perceived as generally effective, procedural complexity, communication gaps, and processing delays remain significant local bottlenecks.

- **Relevance:** This study provides direct empirical evidence from Camarines Sur establishing that local revenue workflows suffer from processing delays and inter-office communication gaps despite using digital tax tools. However, the author analyzed taxpayer perceptions without developing an automated synchronization architecture between municipal offices. The proposed system resolves this operational limitation by providing real-time document routing and payment verification between assessor and treasurer personnel.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _[Abstract & Section 3 Findings, pp. 1–3]:_ Surveys 304 registered enterprises in Libmanan, Camarines Sur, identifying that administrative practices and ETRACS tools face operational friction from procedural complexity and document processing delays.
  - _[Section 4 Discussion & Recommendations, pp. 4–6]:_ Emphasizes that institutional coordination gaps between municipal tax assessment and treasury collection units hinder public transaction efficiency, recommending integrated digital governance mechanisms.
