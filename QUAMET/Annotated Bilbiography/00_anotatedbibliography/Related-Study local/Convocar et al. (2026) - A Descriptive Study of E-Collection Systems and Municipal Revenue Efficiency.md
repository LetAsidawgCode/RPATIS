# A Descriptive Study of E-Collection Systems and Municipal Revenue Efficiency: Evidence from Selected Municipalities in Iloilo Province, Philippines

**Category:** [[Related Study local]]

**[Blessed Art Marie L. Convocar, Marjo F. Feguro, Rachylle Claryce T. Querimit, Irish Faith A. Solinap, Bernandino P. Malang, and Jocelyn DS. Malang. 2026. A Descriptive Study of E-Collection Systems and Municipal Revenue Efficiency: Evidence from Selected Municipalities in Iloilo Province, Philippines. International Journal of Multidisciplinary: Applied Business and Education Research 7, 2 (February 2026), 770–791. DOI:https://doi.org/10.11594/ijmaber.07.02.22]**

- **Summary:** Convocar et al. investigate the operational impact of electronic revenue collection systems across municipal treasurer and accounting offices in Iloilo Province, finding high performance in transparency (mean = 3.96) and processing timeliness (mean = 3.74), but only moderate progress in real-time reconciliation and delinquency tracking due to standalone system deployments.

- **Relevance:** This study provides empirical evidence that standalone municipal revenue platforms fail to achieve real-time record reconciliation when disconnected from core administrative workflows. While e-collection improved transaction transparency, the authors identified that isolated systems maintain data silos. The proposed system resolves this bottleneck by coupling assessment document routing directly with treasury payment ledgers through continuous relational database synchronization.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _[Abstract & Section 4 Results, pp. 770, 778–782]:_ Evaluates municipal finance offices across Iloilo, reporting high ratings for transparency (WM = 3.96) and timeliness (WM = 3.74), but moderate scores for real-time reconciliation.
  - _[Section 5 Discussion & Institutional Barriers, pp. 784–787]:_ Demonstrates that standalone software deployments without cross-departmental data integration prevent automated reconciliation, leaving over 90% of municipal transactions dependent on manual processing.
