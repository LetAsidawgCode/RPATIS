# Revenue generation, collection efficiency, and fiscal sustainability: Evidence from a Philippine municipality

**Category:** [[Related Study local]]

**[Glorina C. Damong. 2026. Revenue generation, collection efficiency, and fiscal sustainability: Evidence from a Philippine municipality. Public and Municipal Finance 15, 1 (January 2026), 14–26. DOI:https://doi.org/10.21511/pmf.15(1).2026.02]**

- **Summary:** Damong evaluates local revenue operations in La Trinidad, Benguet across a ten-year longitudinal dataset (2014–2023) combined with surveys of 301 taxpayers and 24 municipal revenue implementers, discovering that while collection efficiency consistently exceeded annual targets (peaking at 173% in 2021), digital revenue administration remained only moderately adopted due to institutional barriers and manual delinquency tracking.

- **Relevance:** This study demonstrates that municipal revenue operations are constrained by fragmented records and manual delinquency tracking despite achieving target-based collection goals. However, the author evaluated aggregate municipal fiscal metrics without proposing a technical architecture for inter-office record handoffs. The proposed system resolves this operational gap by integrating real-time assessment tracking with automated treasury payment synchronization.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _[Section 3.1 Revenue Trends & Collection Efficiency, pp. 17–19]:_ Analyzes longitudinal municipal finance records from 2014 to 2023, showing revenue expansion from PHP 278M to PHP 690M and collection target fulfillment exceeding 100%.
  - _[Section 4 Challenges to Revenue Strategy Implementation, pp. 21–24]:_ Identifies institutional constraints in digital tax administration, reporting that manual monitoring of delinquencies and delayed inter-office notifications impede sustained local revenue governance.
