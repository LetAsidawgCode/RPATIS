# Effect of the State of Information Communication Technology Usage in the Administration of Property Taxes: A Case Study of Musanze City, Rwanda

**Category:** [[Related Study international]]

**Agnes Wambaire Wangai. 2023. Effect of the State of Information Communication Technology Usage in the Administration of Property Taxes in Rwanda — A Case Study of Musanze City. International Journal of Research and Innovation in Social Science 7, 1 (November 2023). https://dx.doi.org/10.47772/IJRISS.2023.701173**

- **Summary:** Wangai (2023) surveyed 92 respondents comprising commercial building owners and Rwanda Revenue Authority officials in Musanze City using a quantitative research design, establishing a moderate positive correlation between ICT integration and property tax administration efficiency, specifically demonstrating that digitized assessment-to-payment cycles improve data management accuracy and reduce reconciliation lag compared to manual property tax workflows.

- **Relevance:** This empirical study directly supports the proposed system's needs assessment module and Tax Payment Synchronization Module by providing quantified evidence that ICT integration between property assessment registries and payment tracking systems generates measurable reductions in assessment-to-collection processing time in a developing-country municipal context. However, Wangai identified that the Musanze City ICT systems operated as departmentally isolated tools — assessor data management and revenue authority collection records remained on separate platforms — producing recurring reconciliation delays and data security concerns. The proposed system resolves these documented operational constraints by deploying an integrated web architecture that automates assessment document routing and synchronizes property valuation rolls directly with treasury tax collection ledgers in real time, eliminating the inter-departmental data isolation that limited ICT effectiveness in Musanze.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 4.2: Correlation Analysis (p. 7):_ Reports a moderate positive correlation (r = 0.51, p < 0.05) between ICT usage integration and efficiency of property tax administration, specifically between assessment roll digitization and payment cycle speed.
  - _Section 5: Discussion — Challenges of ICT Adoption (pp. 8–9):_ Identifies departmentally siloed tax platforms, data security gaps, and the absence of real-time inter-agency synchronization as primary barriers constraining ICT effectiveness in municipal property tax administration.
