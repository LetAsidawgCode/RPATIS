# Financial Accountability, Trust, and Property Tax Compliance: Experiences from Sub-Saharan Africa

**Category:** [[Related Study international]]

**Kingsley Kwasi Dzagah, Redeemer Krah, and Lexis Alexander Tetteh. 2025. Financial Accountability, Trust, and Property Tax Compliance: Experiences from Sub-Saharan Africa. International Journal of Public Administration (March 2025). https://doi.org/10.1080/01900692.2025.2443026**

- **Summary:** Dzagah et al. (2025) surveyed property owners across Ghanaian municipal assemblies using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) to evaluate tax compliance drivers, establishing that institutional financial accountability directly determines citizen trust and property rate compliance ($\text{Financial Accountability} \rightarrow \text{Trust} \rightarrow \text{Compliance}$) and finding that opaque manual record-keeping severely diminishes municipal revenue mobilization.

- **Relevance:** This empirical study supports the proposed system's Administrative Workflow Panel and tracking monitors by demonstrating that administrative transparency in property records directly dictates compliance and revenue performance in municipal jurisdictions. However, Dzagah et al. evaluated behavioral survey perceptions without implementing a software architecture to eliminate back-office opacity. The proposed system resolves this operational limitation by providing transparent real-time document tracking monitors and synchronized transaction ledgers bridging Municipal Assessor and Treasurer workflows.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Theoretical Framework & Hypotheses (pp. 3–6):_ Stewardship model articulating the structural path linkages connecting local government financial accountability, institutional trust, and property rate compliance.
  - _Results & Structural Model Evaluation (pp. 8–12):_ PLS-SEM path analysis confirming that administrative record transparency significantly elevates tax compliance while disconnected manual record-keeping fosters delinquency.
