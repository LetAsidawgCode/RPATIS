# The Role of Information Technology in Enhancing Property Tax Administration in Decentralised Local Government: A Case Study of Zimbabwe

**Category:** [[Related Study international]]

**Newman Wadesango, Tatenda Nyika, and Lovemore Sitsha. 2024. The Role of Information Technology in Enhancing Property Tax Administration in Decentralised Local Government: A Case Study of Zimbabwe. Journal of Accounting, Finance and Auditing Studies 10, 2 (June 2024), 65–73. https://doi.org/10.56578/jafas100202**

- **Summary:** Wadesango et al. (2024) conducted a quantitative regression study of municipal administrative and finance personnel ($N = 46$) to evaluate information technology adoption in decentralized property tax administration, finding that technological efficiency, revenue contribution, and operational effectiveness account for $80.6\%$ of the variance in tax administration performance ($R^2 = 0.806, F = 88.505, p < 0.001$) while identifying the absence of centralized property databases ($M = 4.317$) and billing software deficiencies ($M = 4.562$) as critical operational barriers.

- **Relevance:** This empirical study justifies the proposed system's Tax Payment Synchronization Module and centralized relational database architecture by quantifying how digital record-keeping directly determines municipal revenue administration efficiency. While Wadesango et al. identified database absences and billing software deficiencies through descriptive surveys, they did not engineer an architectural solution. The proposed system resolves these documented barriers by establishing an integrated relational database linking Assessor valuation rolls directly to Treasurer payment records.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Methodology & Reliability (pp. 66–67):_ Quantitative sampling of local council staff across ICT, accounting, finance, and engineering departments ($N = 46$, Cronbach's $\alpha = 0.996$).
  - _Challenges of ICT Implementation (Table 5, p. 68):_ Survey evaluation identifying the absence of property databases ($M = 4.317, SD = 1.211$) and lack of automated billing software ($M = 4.562, SD = 0.879$) as primary operational constraints.
  - _Regression Model & ANOVA (Tables 8–10, pp. 69–70):_ Multiple regression establishing that ICT efficiency ($\beta = 0.297, p < 0.001$) and revenue contribution ($\beta = 0.640, p < 0.001$) explain $80.6\%$ of property tax administrative success ($R^2 = 0.806, F = 88.505, p < 0.001$).
