# Effect of Property Tax Administration on Local Government Revenue: A Case of Rwanda Revenue Authority in Nyarugenge District, Kigali

**Category:** [[Related Study international]]

**Vedaste Mungwarakarama and Victoire Muhayimana. 2025. Effect of property tax administration on local government revenue: A case of Rwanda revenue authority in Nyarugenge district, Kigali. African Journal of Empirical Research 6, 3 (September 2025), 1077–1093. https://doi.org/10.51867/ajernet.6.3.83**

- **Summary:** Mungwarakarama and Muhayimana (2025) surveyed 398 respondents sampled across 103,985 registered parcels and households in Nyarugenge District, Kigali, using multiple regression analysis to evaluate property tax administration, demonstrating that administrative factors account for 89.3% of the variance in municipal revenue generation ($R = 0.945$, $R^2 = 0.893$, $F = 748.968$, $p < 0.001$), with payment processing ($B = 0.410$) and parcel registration ($B = 0.166$) acting as the strongest drivers.

- **Relevance:** This empirical study supports the proposed system's Tax Payment Synchronization Module and centralized relational database architecture by demonstrating that synchronized parcel registration and payment tracking explain 89.3% of municipal revenue performance. However, Mungwarakarama and Muhayimana examined administrative factors strictly through statistical regression surveys without deploying an automated software solution to reconcile disconnected land registries and collection ledgers. The proposed system resolves this operational disconnect by engineering real-time data synchronization between property assessment records and treasury tax payment tracking across Municipal Assessor and Treasurer offices.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 3 & Abstract (pp. 1077, 1079):_ Survey methodology covering 398 respondents from a target population of 103,985 parcels and households in Nyarugenge District, testing four dimensions: tax character, parcel registration, payment processing, and enforcement.
  - _Section 4.4, Tables 8, 9, 10 & 11 (pp. 1089–1091):_ Regression analysis showing $R = 0.945$, $R^2 = 0.893$ ($F = 748.968, p = 0.000$), where property tax payment ($B = 0.410, t = 9.818$) and property tax registration ($B = 0.166, t = 4.951$) exert significant positive effects on municipal treasury revenues.
