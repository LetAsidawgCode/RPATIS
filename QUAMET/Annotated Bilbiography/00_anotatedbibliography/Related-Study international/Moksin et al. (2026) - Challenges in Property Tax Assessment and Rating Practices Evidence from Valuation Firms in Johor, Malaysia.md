# Challenges in Property Tax Assessment and Rating Practices: Evidence from Valuation Firms in Johor, Malaysia

**Category:** [[Related Study international]]

**Muhammad Alif Firdaus Moksin, Siti Zaleha Daud, and Fitriyah Razali. 2026. Challenges in Property Tax Assessment and Rating Practices: Evidence from Valuation Firms in Johor, Malaysia. International Journal of Research and Innovation in Social Science 10, 6 (June 2026), 2285–2293. https://doi.org/10.47772/IJRISS.2026.100600168**

- **Summary:** Moksin et al. (2026) conducted semi-structured interviews with seven valuation practitioners across three local authority jurisdictions in Johor (Kluang, Segamat, and Kulai) and applied color-coded thematic content analysis, revealing eight operational bottlenecks dominated by inadequate property data (7/7, 100%), technological limitations (6/7, 85.7%), and a shortage of skilled personnel (6/7, 85.7%) while establishing the Operational Valuation Dynamics framework.

- **Relevance:** This empirical study validates the proposed system's needs assessment module by demonstrating that valuation and rating delays stem from fragmented paper records, technological barriers, and departmental data silos rather than calculation formulas alone. However, Moksin et al. restricted their work to qualitative challenge identification without developing an automated software intervention to bridge inter-agency workflows. The proposed system resolves these documented operational constraints by deploying an integrated web architecture providing automated assessment document routing and real-time data synchronization between Municipal Assessor and Treasurer offices.

- **Source Reference(s):**
  - _Section 3, Table 2 & Figure 1 (pp. 2290–2291):_ Frequency analysis and ranking of property tax assessment challenges, showing inadequate property records cited by 7 of 7 practitioners (100%), technological limitations by 6 of 7 (85.7%), and inconsistent cross-municipal procedures by 4 of 7 (57.1%).
  - _Section 4 & Section 5 (pp. 2291–2292):_ Articulation of the Operational Valuation Dynamics framework, noting that manual data collection, lack of inter-departmental data sharing, and procedural discrepancies directly impede municipal assessment roll maintenance.
