B. PROPOSAL OUTLINE
1. PROJECT TITLE
Provide a concise and specific title that identifies the proposed system, its main purpose, and/or its target users.
Proposed title: Needs Assessment for a Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization System: Resolving
Disconnected Records and Workflow Delays in Municipal Assessor and Treasurer Offices in Pasacao
2. PROJECT BACKGROUND / CONTEXT
Explain the current situation or environment where the system will be used. Describe the organization, users, existing
process, and relevant context.
Key points to discuss:
• Current situation or process
• Organization/client context
• Target users
• Relevant observations or evidence
Local government units in the Philippines collect real property taxes as a primary revenue source. This function requires close
administrative coordination among municipal and provincial offices. The Municipal Assessor's Office appraises property
values. The Municipal Treasurer's Office collects tax payments. Under Republic Act 7160, the Provincial Assessor's Office in Pili
holds final approval authority over property assessments and transfers of ownership. In practice, paper-based approval steps
cause long operational delays across these offices.
The target environment includes four municipalities in Camarines Sur: Canaman, Magarao, San Fernando, and Pasacao. These
fourth-class and lower-income municipalities operate with limited computer hardware and small technical teams. Some
paper archives date back to 1949. Pasacao serves as the primary benchmark site. Pasacao operates a local Electronic Real
Property Tax (eRPT) installation for internal assessor records. However, a Transfer of Assessment in Pasacao requires more
than seven days. In Canaman and Magarao, personnel manage all records on paper. An assessment of a new building or a
transfer of title requires 7 to 14 days because staff must physically transport paper forms to the Provincial Capitol in Pili for
final approval.
Target users:
Municipal Assessor's Office personnel: Staff who encode property assessments and assemble title documents.
Municipal Treasurer's Office personnel: Staff who review tax payments and issue financial clearances.
Provincial Assessor's Office personnel: Officers in Pili who examine ownership documents and grant final statutory
approvals.
Municipal administrators and supervisors: Officers who monitor transaction queues across offices.
The research team observed processing workflows and interviewed municipal staff during the needs assessment phase.
Neither office has real-time visibility into transaction statuses. Documents stop in manual queues. Staff spend hours verifying
whether a taxpayer paid property tax. These conditions match the findings of Chinilla and Lagura (2025). Their study confirms
that paper records cause administrative delays in municipal offices. Sunirah et al. (2026) also show that isolated tax
applications do not reduce workflow backlogs.
3. STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM
Identify the specific problems, limitations, inefficiencies, or unmet needs that the proposed system will address.
Main Problem: The lack of a shared digital platform forces municipal and provincial offices to use manual procedures.
Consequently, assessment processing requires 7 to 14 days, and staff must manually cross-check records and physically
transport documents to Pili for final approval.
Specific Problems:
1. Disconnected Departmental Databases: The Assessor's Office and the Treasurer's Office keep records in separate systems
or paper ledgers. Staff must carry physical documents or call other offices to reconcile record discrepancies.
2. Document Processing Latency: A Transfer of Assessment in Pasacao requires more than seven days because staff transfer
paper documents manually. In Canaman, title transfers and building assessments take 7 to 14 days because staff transport
paper forms to Pili for provincial approval.
3. Manual Provincial Approval Process: The Provincial Assessor's Office in Pili holds statutory final approval for property
assessments and ownership title changes. Currently, municipal offices rely on physical couriers to deliver paper packets to
Pili, which causes severe transit delays.
4. Lack of Document Tracking Visibility: Offices cannot track the location of an assessment document in the processing
queue. Frontline staff must make phone calls or search file cabinets to answer taxpayer questions.
5. Legacy Paper Records: Some municipalities store paper records that date back to 1949. These physical binders
deteriorate over time. They slow down verification tasks and make record updates difficult.
6. Manual Cross-Checking: Treasurer personnel manually compare tax payment entries against assessor rolls. This manual
process takes many work hours and increases error rates during tax collection periods.
4. PROPOSED SOLUTION
Describe the proposed system and explain how it will address the identified problems. Give a clear overview of what the
system will do.
System overview: The proposed system is a web-based Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization
System. The system uses a three-tier web architecture. It provides a shared web application for municipal offices and an
endorsement portal for the Provincial Assessor's Office in Pili.
The platform contains three functional tiers:
Presentation Tier: Provides role-based browser screens. These include assessor evaluation screens, treasurer validation
views, a provincial approval portal, and a document tracking monitor.
Application Tier: Manages business logic. This tier routes assessment documents automatically, controls user permissions
with Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), links valuations to tax records, and manages digital provincial endorsements.
Data Tier: Stores all records in a centralized relational database. This tier maintains property assessment data, ownership
title attachments, transaction logs, and audit records.
The system will operate in parallel with existing office procedures during pilot testing. This parallel run protects data integrity
and validates software stability. In compliance with Republic Act 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012), developers will use masked
dummy data during system development and testing.
5. PROJECT OBJECTIVES
State what the project intends to accomplish. Objectives should be specific, measurable where possible, and directly related
to the identified problems.
General Objective: Design, develop, and evaluate a web-based Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office
Synchronization System that connects municipal assessor and treasurer offices, automates document routing to the provincial
office, and reduces processing delays.
Specific Objectives:
1. Conduct a needs assessment across Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao, and San Fernando to measure baseline processing
times and record system requirements.
2. Design and develop a three-tier web platform with assessor screens, treasurer validation views, a provincial digital
approval module, and a centralized database.
3. Implement an inter-office web portal that provides real-time document tracking, eliminates physical courier trips to Pili,
and removes manual cross-checking between offices.
4. Evaluate the software under ISO/IEC 25010 standards for Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability, Security,
and Reliability, and measure reductions in processing time.
6. SCOPE AND DELIMITATIONS
Define what the proposed system will cover and what it will not cover. Clearly state the boundaries of the project.
Scope:
The system covers property assessment tracking, document routing, payment synchronization, provincial digital approval,
and administrative monitoring.
The study covers four municipalities in Camarines Sur: Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao, and San Fernando, with provincial
endorsement review in Pili.
Baseline data collection involves time-motion measurements of current assessment, tax collection, and provincial
approval workflows.
The system supports property assessment creation, Transfer of Assessment tracking, ownership title verification,
provincial approval, and payment status updates.
The development process uses an Agile method with two-week sprint cycles.
The project evaluation runs from July 2026 to December 2026.
The software runs on standard computers connected to a municipal network and allows web access for provincial
approvers.
Delimitations / Exclusions:
The system does not process online payments or connect to external financial payment gateways.
The system does not rebuild the internal enterprise database of the Provincial Capitol in Pili; it provides a web-based
review and approval portal.
The system excludes business permits, civil registry records, and general municipal accounting.
Evaluation results reflect conditions in lower-class municipalities and may not apply to large urban cities.
The project does not use live citizen property records during development and testing, in compliance with Republic Act
10173.

8. SIGNIFICANCE / BENEFITS OF THE PROJECT
Explain the expected benefits of the proposed system to the client, users, organization, community, or other stakeholders.
Expected benefits:
Assessor's Office Personnel: Staff do not need to carry physical tax forms or search old paper archives. Personnel encode
records once and track each document automatically through the routing pipeline.
Treasurer's Office Personnel: Real-time record synchronization removes the need to cross-check ledgers manually. The
system validates payment records automatically.
Provincial Assessor's Office Personnel: Officers review ownership documents and grant approvals digitally. This process
eliminates courier trips and lost paper files.
Property Owners and Local Residents: The system reduces processing delays for transactions like a Transfer of
Assessment. Citizens make fewer office visits to track document status.
Local Government Units: The software provides an auditable digital workflow. This workflow supports national e-
governance policies under Republic Act 12001 and the Digital Government Masterplan 2023–2028.
Future IT Researchers: The study provides measured performance data and an architectural model for inter-office
synchronization in rural government offices.
Academic Institutions: The project supports the Institutional Research Agenda of the University of Nueva Caceres
regarding digital public service transformation.
9. EXISTING SYSTEM / CURRENT PROCESS
Describe how the task or service is currently performed. Identify existing tools, procedures, manual processes, or software
being used.
Current process description: In Pasacao, the Municipal Assessor's Office uses an internal eRPT software tool. This tool does
not connect to the Treasurer's Office. When a citizen requests a Transfer of Assessment, assessor staff print paper forms. Staff
physically deliver these forms to the Treasurer's Office. Treasurer personnel verify payment history against a separate ledger.
The documents then return to the Assessor's Office for final encoding. This manual exchange takes more than seven days.
In Canaman, Magarao, and San Fernando, municipal offices do not use digital tax applications. Staff process all property
records and tax clearances on paper. To assess a new building or transfer title of ownership, personnel encode paper forms
and send them to the Provincial Capitol in Pili. This approval process requires 7 to 14 days because physical couriers transport
paper folders. Staff in both municipal offices cross-check assessment rolls and collection ledgers by hand.
None of the four municipalities have a shared document tracking dashboard or automated inter-office routing to the
provincial level. Staff rely on telephone calls and office visits to check document status. Documents sometimes stop in transit
without notice.

11. SYSTEM REQUIREMENTS
Identify the expected functional and non-functional requirements.
Functional requirements: What the system must do.
1. The system must allow assessor staff to create property assessment records and attach ownership title documents
through a web interface.
2. The system must route submitted assessment records to the Treasurer's Office queue automatically.
3. The system must provide a treasurer interface to review assessment documents and record clearances.
4. The system must route cleared assessment packets to the Provincial Assessor's Office queue in Pili for final review.
5. The system must allow provincial assessor officers to inspect ownership documents and record final digital approvals.
6. The system must display a real-time dashboard that shows transaction status to all participating offices.
7. The system must match property assessment files with tax payment entries and flag mismatches.
8. The system must restrict functions and data views based on assigned user roles.
9. The system must record an audit log with timestamps and user identities for all document actions.
10. The system must support a parallel run mode that works alongside current manual office workflows.
Non-functional requirements: Performance, security, usability, reliability, compatibility, etc.
1. Performance Efficiency: The system must load dashboard views and routing updates within three seconds on local and
remote network connections.
2. Usability: Staff must be able to complete routine encoding, validation, and approval tasks within five user actions.
3. Security: The system must encrypt stored property records, use HTTPS for data transfer, and enforce session timeouts.
4. Reliability: The system must use atomic database transactions to prevent incomplete data updates.
5. Compatibility: The web application must function on Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, and Microsoft Edge without extra
plugins.
6. Functional Suitability: The software must perform all defined routing, matching, and approval operations without logic
errors.
12. TECHNOLOGY / PLATFORM
Identify the proposed hardware, software, programming languages, frameworks, database, platforms, APIs, or other
technologies.
Proposed technologies:
Hardware: Standard desktop computers in municipal offices and the Provincial Capitol, connected via local and wide area
networks.
Software / Tools: Standard web browsers (Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge) and a secure web application
server.
Programming / Frameworks: PHP (Laravel or CodeIgniter framework) for backend logic, with HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript
for user interfaces.
Database: MySQL or MariaDB relational database for property records, payment logs, and audit data.
Platform: Web application deployed on a municipal server with secure remote portal access for provincial approvers.

14. DEVELOPMENT METHODOLOGY
Identify and briefly explain the development methodology or framework to be used (e.g., Agile, Scrum, Waterfall, Prototype,
RAD). Explain why it is appropriate for the project.
Methodology: Agile Development Model (two-week sprint cycles)
Reason for selection: The Agile model allows the team to adapt software features based on ongoing feedback from municipal
personnel and provincial approvers. The project begins with a field needs assessment that uncovers specific workflow
requirements across offices. Two-week sprints let developers deliver testable software increments regularly. Municipal and
provincial staff can test each increment, such as the routing engine, treasurer validation view, and provincial approval module,
before the team builds subsequent features. This approach reduces design errors before pilot deployment. It also supports
the mixed-methods research design through regular evaluation intervals.
