Christian Louise Asido       ​ Module 1 : eRPT Assessment, Valuation, and Tax Declaration Engine 
 
1.​ Project Overview and Stakeholder List 
Our project group is developing an automated web platform titled as Needs Assessment for a Real Property 
Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office Synchronization System: Resolving Disconnected Records and 
Workflow Delays in Municipal Assessor and Treasurer Offices in Pasacao. The main goal of our study is to 
conduct a needs assessment and comparative evaluation of local municipal workflows across Canaman, 
Magarao, San Fernando and benchmarking with Pasacao (which uses an existing eRPT setup) to solve 
real-world problems in local government units. Specifically, our project addresses the severe administrative 
frictions, valuation delays, fragmented record-keeping, and  the massive operational headache of migrating 
decades-old legacy records dating back to 1949. 
This document covers Module 1: eRPT Assessment, Valuation, and Tax Declaration Engine. This module 
handles property record intake, automated valuation computation, digital Tax Declaration (TD) generation, and 
first-stage endorsement of assessment documents. The module extends the eRPT system that Pasacao 
currently uses. It addresses three specific gaps found during field interviews: incomplete building-classification 
data fields, missing automated valuation computation for certain property types, and no online document 
pre-screening workflow.​

2.​ Information-gathering methods used and summary of findings 
 
Field Interviews : The team conducted structured interviews with assessor staff and tax mappers at the 
Canaman and Pasacao Municipal Assessor's Offices using a prepared interview guide covering office setup, 
daily workflows, tax computation, bottlenecks, record keeping, and taxpayer experience. 
Document Analysis : The team reviewed physical assessment forms and request forms such as the 
Certificate of Land Holding, Certified True Copy of Tax Declaration , and Transfer of Assessment form. The 
team also reviewed the provincial Schedule of Market Values ordinance structure as described by the 
interviewees. 
Comparative Observation : The team compared the manual workflow of Canaman (Excel spreadsheets and 
paper ledgers going back to 1949) against the partially automated eRPT workflow of Pasacao. 
Professor Consultation : The team held two consultation sessions with the Research Adviser and the 
Capstone Professor. The sessions confirmed the research scope and identified the module's primary 
differentiator from other GIS-based capstone groups. 
 
Key Findings: 
 
Canaman: 
●​ The Canaman office has five staff members. They handle an average of three walk-in transfers per day and 15 
transfers per month. Certifications are the most common daily transaction. 
●​ Staff store property records on Excel spreadsheets and in physical ledger books. Some records date back to 1949. 
Retrieving a historical record takes 30 minutes to three days, depending on the record age. 
●​ Staff compute valuation manually using the formula prescribed under Republic Act No. 7160 (Local Government 
Code of 1991): Area multiplied by the Unit Value from the Schedule of Market Values produces the Fair Market 
Value. The Fair Market Value multiplied by the applicable Assessment Level percentage produces the Assessed 
Value. The office has a basic computer system, but it has gaps. Certain building sub-components are not yet 
encodable in the current system, which causes incomplete assessment records. 
●​ Assessment levels follow a provincial Schedule of Market Values ordinance. The ordinance covers Residential (1st, 
2nd, and 3rd class), Commercial, Agricultural, and Machinery property types, each with separate unit values and 
assessment level percentages. 
●​ The discount and penalty structure is standard. Taxpayers who pay by December of the current year receive a 20% 
discount. Taxpayers who pay in the first quarter of the following year receive a 10% discount. Late payments incur a 
2% penalty per month of delay. 
●​ The Municipal Assessor's Office issues only a Recommending Approval. The Provincial Assessor's Office in Pili 
issues the final approval. Municipal staff physically travel to the Provincial Capitol once a week, usually on Thursday 
or Friday, to transmit new documents and retrieve approved copies. 
●​ Before the Assessor's Office issues a complete Tax Declaration for a building, it must coordinate with the 
Engineering Office for occupancy permits and building plans, and with the Municipal Planning and Development 
Office for zoning certificates when a reclassification request is involved. 
 
 
Pasacao: 
●​ The Pasacao office uses an eRPT system. The system was installed in 2022 and updated in 2024. 
●​ The office handles 50 to 100 certification requests per day. Document verification takes about five minutes per 
request. Appraisal takes up to seven days for complex transactions. 
●​ Despite using the eRPT system, staff still prepare assessments on paper first. Staff then manually encode the data 
into the system. The system does not allow online document submission or pre-screening. This creates a 
duplicate-effort workflow. 
●​ The most common request types are: Tax Declaration, Certificate of Land Holding, Certified True Copy of Tax 
Declaration, Transfer and Cancellation, Certificate of No Improvement, and Effectivity Certificate. 
●​ After staff encode property data, the eRPT system computes the assessment level and annual tax automatically. 
This automated computation is one of the key functions the proposed module must retain and extend. 
●​ A known gap in the Pasacao eRPT system is that certain data fields required for accurate assessment are missing 
from the system's encoding forms. This causes incomplete records and requires staff to work around the system. 
●​ No public portal exists. Taxpayers cannot check document requirements or submission status online. Staff 
confirmed that an online pre-screening function would reduce the number of return visits by taxpayers who arrive 
with incomplete documents. 
 
 
Professors Consultation(Sir Chin and Sir June Areb) 
●​ June Areb confirmed that the module should extend and improve upon the eRPT system's existing functions rather 
than replace it. He recommended building the module as a service layer that connects to the existing eRPT 
infrastructure through an application programming interface. 
●​ Chin directed the team to address local municipal workflow efficiency before considering provincial-level 
bottlenecks. 
●​ Both professors confirmed that automated Tax Declaration generation, assessment computation, and online 
pre-screening of requirements are valid primary functions for Module 1. 
 
 
3.​ Functional requirements (prioritized) 
 
MUST HAVE 
●​ The system must accept and store property assessment request submissions. Each submission must capture the 
property type (Land, Building, Machinery, or Other Improvement), classification (Residential, Commercial, 
Agricultural, or Industrial), and Property Index Number. 
●​ The system must allow staff to search existing property records by Owner Name, Property Index Number, 
Assessment Roll Number, Barangay, and Lot or Block Number. The system must return results in five seconds or 
less. 
●​ The system must compute Fair Market Value automatically. The computation must follow the formula under 
Republic Act No. 7160, Section 198: Area multiplied by the Unit Value from the encoded Schedule of Market Values 
equals the Fair Market Value. 
●​ The system must compute Assessed Value automatically. The computation must follow Republic Act No. 7160, 
Section 199: Fair Market Value multiplied by the applicable Assessment Level percentage equals the Assessed 
Value. Assessment level percentages must match the property class as the Local Government Code and the 
provincial Schedule of Market Values ordinance mandate. 
●​ The system must support all standard assessment transaction types: New Assessment or Discovery, Transfer of 
Assessment, Revision or Re-assessment, Reclassification, Cancellation, and Subdivision or Consolidation. 
●​ The system must generate a digital Tax Declaration. The Tax Declaration must include: full property details, 
computed Fair Market Value, computed Assessed Value, effective date, property classification, and the Municipal 
Assessor's Recommending Approval notation. 
●​ The system must check that all required documents are present before the request moves to the appraisal or 
verification stage. For a Transfer of Assessment, the system requires a Deed of Absolute Sale (or equivalent 
conveyance document). For a new building assessment, the system requires a Building Permit and a Certificate of 
Occupancy. 
●​ The system must allow staff to encode all building sub-components. Sub-components include: structural type, floor 
area, year built, depreciation rate, and accessories or additional improvements. This requirement addresses the 
encoding gap found in the Canaman field interview. 
 
SHOULD HAVE 
●​ The system should allow taxpayers or authorized representatives to submit initial assessment requirements online. 
The system should check the document checklist and flag missing items. It should notify the taxpayer of any missing 
documents before the taxpayer visits the office. 
●​ The system should compute the annual Real Property Tax due automatically. The computation must follow Republic 
Act No. 7160, Section 233: Basic Real Property Tax equals Assessed Value multiplied by the applicable tax rate (not 
exceeding 1% for provinces). The computation must also include the Special Education Fund levy of 1% of the 
Assessed Value, applicable discounts (20% for advance payment by December of the current year; 10% for 
first-quarter payment of the following year), and penalties (2% per month of delay, compounded per quarter). 
●​ The system should store the complete assessment history of each property. The history must include all prior Tax 
Declarations, revisions, reclassifications, and recorded owners from the earliest available declaration. Staff must be 
able to view this history in a property timeline panel. 
●​ The system should generate a Notice of Assessment document automatically when a Tax Declaration is final. The 
system must provide this document to the taxpayer as official notification of the new assessed value. 
●​ The system should produce a formatted Assessment Roll entry. The system must allow staff to export this entry and 
transmit it to the Treasurer's Office as a digital record. 
 
 
COULD HAVE 
●​ The system could allow staff to attach scanned supporting documents to the property record. Documents include: 
Deed of Sale, Land Title, Building Permit, and Certificate of Occupancy. 
●​ The system could display a taxpayer-facing document checklist page. The page must show the documents required 
per transaction type. Account registration must not be required to view this page. 
●​ The system could flag properties where the encoded digital record does not match the paper-based historical 
record. The system must prompt a reconciliation workflow for flagged properties. 
 
 
WONT HAVE 
●​ This Module will not process online tax payments or connect to payment gateways. Those functions belong to the 
Treasurer's Office scope (Module 2). 
●​ The system will not include GIS or tax mapping functions. Those functions belong to a separate capstone group to 
avoid scope conflict. 
●​ This Module will not replace the formal physical signing process at the Provincial Capitol in Pili. That function is the 
scope of Module 3.

5.​ Key use cases or user stories (3–5 high-priority ones) 
 
New Building Assessment (Tax Declaration Generation):​
As an Assessor's Office staff member, I want to encode a new building's complete details into the system and 
have it compute the Fair Market Value, Assessed Value, and Real Property Tax automatically, so that I can 
generate an accurate digital Tax Declaration within minutes instead of preparing it manually over several hours. 
 
Transfer of Assessment with Online Pre-Screening: 
As a real property owner who needs to transfer a Tax Declaration to a new owner's name, I want to check and 
submit my documentary requirements online before visiting the Assessor's Office, so that I arrive with complete 
documents and avoid making multiple return trips. 
Historical Property Record Lookup: 
As an Assessor's Office staff member, I want to search for a property's complete assessment history using the 
owner's name or Property Index Number, so that I can verify prior assessments and retrieve records going 
back to 1949 within seconds instead of spending 30 minutes to three days searching physical ledgers. 
Annual Real Property Tax Computation with Discounts and Penalties:​
As an Assessor's Office staff member, I want the system to automatically compute a property's annual Real 
Property Tax due including any applicable discount or penalty based on the payment date so that I can give the 
taxpayer an accurate billing breakdown on demand without performing the calculation manually. 
 
6.​ Assumptions, constraints, and open issues 
 
 
ASSUMPTIONS 
●​ The Assessor's Office will provide the Schedule of Market Values ordinance data for encoding into the valuation 
database. The Pasacao interview confirmed that the ordinance exists in both book and system form. 
●​ The Municipal Assessor will participate in system evaluation and will confirm the Recommending Approval workflow 
representation. 
●​ The team will use mock or masked property data during development and testing to comply with Republic Act No. 
10173. The team will use actual property data only during the supervised field evaluation phase. 
●​ Internet connectivity at the target offices is adequate for a web-based system. 
●​ The system covers only the assessment functions of the Assessor's Office. Real Property Tax collection and 
payment processing remain with the Treasurer's Office (Module 2). 
●​ During the pilot phase, the Provincial Capitol approval step remains a physical process. Module 1 generates only 
the Recommending Approval packet for endorsement. Module 3 handles digital approval routing. 
 
 
CONSTRAINTS 
TYPE 
Assessment levels and tax rates follow Republic Act No. 7160 (Local Government Code) and 
the provincial Schedule of Market Values ordinance. The system cannot change these rates 
without a new ordinance from the Province.​ 
Policy 
All municipal Assessors in Camarines Sur operate under the Provincial Assessor's authority. 
Only the Provincial Assessor issues the final approval signature on Tax Declarations. The 
system produces only a Recommending Approval package at the municipal level in Module 1. 
Legal and Policy 
 
The eRPT system used in the target municipalities is a locally developed and customized 
system. This module extends it as a service layer. The team will not change the core codebase 
of the existing eRPT system. The Capstone Professor recommended this approach to maintain 
institutional compatibility.​
 
Technical 
The academic project timeline runs from July to December 2026. The deliverable for this phase 
is a working prototype. The complete production system is scheduled for the following 
academic year's capstone continuation.​
 
Time 
The Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act (Republic Act No. 12001) targets full 
implementation in 2028. Current assessment computations must follow the existing Schedule 
of Market Values framework, not the reform act framework.​ 
Regulatory 
Legacy records from 1949 to approximately 2000 in Canaman exist only in physical ledger 
books. Digitizing these records requires manual data entry by assessor staff. Historical 
retrieval in the system is limited to records that staff encode during the pilot run.​

OPEN ISSUES 
●​ Will the Assessor's Office provide an exported digital dataset (such as a spreadsheet or database export) of the 
Schedule of Market Values ordinance, or must the development team manually encode the unit values and 
classification tables from the physical ordinance book into the system database? 
●​ The system must handle requests where the person at the counter is not the property owner. Those cases require a 
Special Power of Attorney or a written Authorization Letter. The team must confirm the exact verification logic with 
the Assessor's Office. 
●​ A Transfer of Assessment requires a Certificate Authorizing Registration from the Bureau of Internal Revenue. The 
system currently plans to accept a scanned upload only. Whether any real-time verification is feasible within the 
project timeline is not yet decided. 
●​ The team and the Assessor's Office must agree on how long the office will run both the existing workflow and the 
new system at the same time. This comparison period provides data for the Paired t-test evaluation. A duration of 
two weeks is proposed. 
●​ The team must confirm the exact Assessment Roll format that the Treasurer's Office expects. This ensures that the 
Module 1 export function produces a compatible file. 
 
7.​ Attachment of Data Gathering Evidences 
 
Sample Interview Questions 
1.​ Can you walk us through the step-by-step process from the moment a taxpayer arrives at your service counter until 
they receive their finished document or clearance? 
2.​ What specific documentary requirements must property owners submit for each of the following major transactions? 
a.​ New Tax Declaration: Requirements for newly declared land, new buildings/houses, or machinery. 
b.​ Transfer of Ownership: Requirements when transferring a property (such as Deed of Sale, BIR tax 
clearance, transfer tax receipt, or previous clearances). 
c.​ Reassessment of Property: Requirements when an owner requests a value correction, property 
reclassification, or physical inspection. 
d.​ Issuance of Clearances: Requirements for getting a Tax Clearance, Certificate of No Property, or Certificate 
of No Improvement. 
3.​ How do you compute real property taxes in your office from start to finish? 
a.​ Market Value & Assessed Value: How do you determine market value, and what assessment percentages 
are applied based on classification (residential, agricultural, commercial)? 
b.​ Tax Rates: What tax percentages are applied to get the annual tax due (such as the 1% basic tax and 1% 
Special Education Fund)? 
c.​ Discounts and Penalties: What discounts are given for prompt or advance payments, and what interest or  
penalties are charged for late payments? 
4.​ How do personnel search for an existing property record, tax declaration, or property identification number when a 
client inquires at the counter? 
5.​ On average, how long does it take to complete one full transaction from initial queuing to document release? 
Roughly how many minutes are spent on: 
a.​ Requirement Checking: Checking if the client's submitted papers are complete. 
b.​ Record Search: Locating the property in physical record books, maps, or file folders. 
c.​ Value & Tax Computation: Calculating the assessed value and tax amounts. 
d.​ Approval & Release: Review, official signature, municipal seal, and releasing the document. 
6.​ How often do calculation mistakes or clerical errors happen during manual tax computations, what usually causes 
them, and how are they corrected? 
7.​ If an online portal allowed taxpayers to check requirements, submit initial papers, and see tax estimates before 
visiting, which tasks would this reduce or simplify for your staff?
