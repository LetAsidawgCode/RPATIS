Target Office: Municipal Assessor's Office — Municipality of San Fernando, Camarines Sur
Research Project: Needs Assessment and Development of an Automated Real Property Tax Assessment and Digital Collection Framework
for the Municipality of San Fernando: Optimizing Local Revenue Generation and Taxpayer Compliance Workflows
Student Researchers:
Rizalina A. Ortega  |  John Kenneth P. Calubad  |  Christian Louise O. Asido
Interview Purpose:
To understand your daily office workflows, requirements, manual tax computation steps, and counter
bottlenecks to design a helpful and easy-to-use web portal for the Assessor's Office.
Confidentiality Notice:
All information shared will be kept strictly confidential and used solely for academic research in full compliance
with the Data Privacy Act (Republic Act No. 10173).
Note for the Interviewee: This interview is conducted by the student research team to gather firsthand practical insights. A printed copy is
provided for your reference and review during our discussion.

PART 4 — RECORD KEEPING AND NATIONAL RULES
15. How are important physical documents — such as property assessment sheets, tax maps, and record books — organized and
protected against loss, wear, fire, or water damage?
16. Does your office currently use any software program, computerized spreadsheet, or electronic database for recording or calculating
assessments? If yes, what is it and what does it do?
17. Are there any recent national government guidelines or new property valuation rules that your office has started preparing for or
implementing?
18. Where and in what format is your municipality's official Schedule of Market Values kept and referenced in your daily work (such as

PART 5 — PUBLIC SERVICE AND TAXPAYER EXPERIENCE
19. What are the most frequent questions, complaints, or points of confusion that property owners express regarding assessments and
clearances?
20. Approximately how much of your staff's daily time is spent answering basic questions (such as asking for requirements or checking
status) from walk-in clients or phone inquiries?
21. Have property owners or taxpayers expressed interest in being able to check requirements, track their application status, or
estimate their tax dues online from home?
PART 6 — FEEDBACK ON THE PROPOSED ONLINE SYSTEM
22. Has your office tried using any digital tools, online forms, or messaging channels for public services in the past? If yes, what worked
well and what challenges came up?
23. What concerns or challenges do you foresee if a web-based pre-assessment system is introduced (such as internet stability, staff
computer comfort, data privacy, or daily workflow adjustments)?
24. If an online portal allowed taxpayers to check requirements, submit initial papers, and see tax estimates before visiting, which tasks
would this reduce or simplify for your staff?
25. Would your office be open to testing the prototype portal with our team (such as running 15 to 20 sample assessments on the
system alongside your manual books) to check its speed and calculation accuracy?
26. Are there any local rules, municipal tax ordinances, or unique practices in the Municipality of San Fernando that our research team
should know about and build into the system?
