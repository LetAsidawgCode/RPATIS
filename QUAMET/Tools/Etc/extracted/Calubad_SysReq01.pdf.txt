 
1. Project Overview and Stakeholder List 
 
1.1 Full Overview of the Group System 
Local Government Units (LGUs) across Camarines Sur rely on real property 
taxation (RPT) as their primary statutory foundation for local fiscal autonomy 
under Title II of Republic Act No. 7160 (Local Government Code of 1991). 
However, municipal tax operations are crippled by severe operational frictions: 
fragmented departmental record-keeping, manual property valuation prone to 
human calculation errors, decades-old physical archives dating back to 1949, 
and an entrenched physical dependency on the Provincial Capitol in Pili for 
mandatory statutory approvals. To solve these systemic bottlenecks, our 
research group proposes the Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office 
Synchronization System (RPAT-IOSS), an integrated 3-tier web platform designed 
to eliminate inter-office data silos and compress property assessment and 
ownership transfer turnaround times from over 7–14 days down to less than 24 
hours. The platform comprises three interlocking subsystems: 
eRPT Assessment, Valuation & Tax Declaration Engine — Automates statutory base 
market value appraisal, assessment level formulas, and electronic Tax 
Declaration (TD), Notice of Assessment (NOA), and Property Appraisal Schedule 
(PAAS) generation, mitigating calculation errors and multi-day paper 
preparation. 
Inter-Office Treasury Synchronization & Ledger Reconciliation Module — 
Dynamically synchronizes approved municipal assessment rolls with treasury 
collection databases in real time, computing statutory RPT dues, early-bird 
discounts (20%/10%), and delinquency penalties (2%/month) while instantly 
generating electronic tax clearances. 
(Individual Focus): Provincial Capitol Remote Endorsement & Digital Approval 
Pipeline — Establishes a secure, authenticated digital review, annotation, and 
approval bridge connecting component municipal assessors directly with the 
Provincial Assessor's Office at the Provincial Capitol in Pili, eliminating 
physical road transit and cutting multi-week provincial approval queues. 
 
 
 
1.2 Subsystem Focus: Provincial Capitol Remote Endorsement & Digital Approval 
Pipeline 
Under RA 7160 and Camarines Sur provincial administration rules, municipal 
assessors in component municipalities (such as Pasacao, Canaman, Magarao, and 
San Fernando) hold only recommending approval authority. Final approval and 
statutory issuance of the Certificate of Inspection and Survey (CIS) and new 
Tax Declarations rest exclusively with the Provincial Assessor at the 
Provincial Capitol in Pili. Currently, municipal personnel must physically 
travel by road to the Capitol once a week to submit paper dossiers and retrieve 
endorsements from prior weeks. Busier municipalities wait between 2 to 3 weeks 
 
 
because the Capitol manually inspects files from 35 component municipalities. 
This subsystem digitizes this inter-governmental bottleneck: municipal 
assessors upload certified digital dossiers; provincial examiners inspect and 
annotate files online; the Provincial Assessor applies cryptographic approval 
stamps; and finalized endorsements and CIS documents are instantly dispatched 
back to the LGU in real time.

2. Information-Gathering Methods Used and Summary of Findings 
2.1 Techniques Employed 
To establish empirical grounding, four complementary information-gathering 
techniques were executed across target LGUs and academic consultations: 
Semi-Structured Field Interviews: Conducted structured on-site interviews with 
key informants: the Municipal Assessor Office Head and 4 staff members at 
Canaman LGU, Canaman Municipal Treasury personnel, and Tax Mapper I (John 
Francisco) alongside assessment personnel at Pasacao LGU. Investigated 
transaction frequencies, documentary requirements, calculation practices, and 
provincial routing. 
Field Observation & Workflow Walkthrough: Directly observed citizen intake, 
counter queue dynamics, spreadsheet lookup methods, physical archive folder 
retrieval, and the manual bundling of paper dossiers destined for the weekly 
car run to the Provincial Capitol in Pili. 
Faculty & Academic Consultations: Participated in technical consultation 
sessions with research advisers (Sir Chin, Sir Arreb) and CSACIS capstone 
faculty to establish project boundaries, validate the provincial endorsement 
bottleneck, and ensure distinct project uniqueness. 
2.2 Summary of Key Findings 
The Provincial Courier Bottleneck: Municipal assessors in Camarines Sur do not 
have final approval authority. Endorsements must be approved by the Provincial 
Capitol in Pili. Municipal staff courier documents by vehicle once a week 
(Thursdays/Fridays). While Canaman takes ~1 week due to lower volume, busier 
municipalities wait 2 to 3 weeks because the Capitol services 35 component 
municipalities. 
The Pasacao Computerization Gap: While Pasacao utilizes localized software 
(eRPT, upgraded 2024) that reduced routine certifications to 5–15 minutes, 
property transfers still take up to 7 working days. The system remains 
completely internal to assessor workstations, lacking citizen visibility and 
dynamic integration with the Provincial Capitol and Municipal Treasury. 
Departmental Data Silos: Assessor and Treasurer offices operate in complete 
isolation. Assessors generate assessment rolls on paper/Excel, while the 
Treasury manually tracks payments. Citizens must physically shuttle receipts 
between windows to verify tax status and obtain clearances. 
Archival Search Latency: In manual LGUs like Canaman, active records take 10–30 
minutes to retrieve, but retrieving historical records dating back to 1949 
 
 
takes 2 to 3 full business days in physical archive vaults, with severe risks 
of document decay.

5. Key Use Cases or User Stories (High Priority) 
The operational workflows of the subsystem are specified through three 
high-priority use cases and five prioritized user stories: 
5.1 Use Case 1: Municipal Assessor Bundles and Submits Remote Endorsement (UC-01) 
Actor: Municipal Assessor / Assessment Appraiser 
Goal: Package and transmit an evaluated property assessment dossier to the 
Provincial Capitol digitally without physical road transit. 
Preconditions: Municipal assessor has completed local appraisal and logged in 
with valid credentials. 
Main Steps: 1. Assessor navigates to 'Capitol Endorsement' module. 2. Selects 
pending transaction (e.g., Transfer of Assessment). 3. Uploads certified 
digital documents (scanned Deed of Sale, ROD Title, Field Appraisal Form, Lot 
Sketch <= 25MB). 4. System validates file integrity and generates SHA-256 hash. 
5. Assessor clicks 'Submit Recommending Endorsement'. 6. System marks status 
'Under Provincial Review' and queues docket in Capitol inbox. 
 Postconditions: Endorsement packet is queued in Provincial Capitol inbox with 
an immutable timestamp, visible on municipal tracking dashboards. 
5.2 Use Case 2: Provincial Review Examiner Inspects and Annotates Docket (UC-02 
Actor: Provincial Technical Review Examiner 
Goal: Perform structured compliance auditing and digital annotation on submitted 
municipal dockets. 
Preconditions: Docket is in 'Under Provincial Review' status; examiner has 
active Capitol reviewer credentials. 
Main Steps: 1. Examiner selects pending municipal docket from provincial queue. 
2. System displays split-screen view: digitized PDF dossier viewer on left, 
statutory compliance checklist on right. 3. Examiner audits property 
boundaries, land classification, and conveyance deeds. 4. Marks compliance 
checkboxes. If deficiency is found: examiner highlights error, adds structured 
corrective remark, and clicks 'Return for Revision' (status changes to 
 
 
'Deficiency Flagged' with instant notification). 5. If compliant: examiner 
clicks 'Endorse for Final Approval'. 
Postconditions:Docket is approved for signature and forwarded to the Provincial 
Assessor's final signing queue. 
5.3 Use Case 3: Provincial Assessor Applies Digital Approval and Issues CIS 
(UC-03) 
Actor: Provincial Assessor / Authorized Approving Official 
Goal: Affix cryptographic digital approval, generate official CIS, and dispatch 
finalized endorsement to the LGU. 
Preconditions: Docket has been audited and recommended for approval by the 
Provincial Review Examiner. 
Main Steps: 1. Provincial Assessor opens recommended docket and inspects 
examiner's compliance summary. 2. Clicks 'Authorize & Execute Digital 
Approval'. 3. System prompts for authentication PIN/2FA signing token. 4. 
System affixes cryptographic signature, generates verification QR code, and 
compiles official Certificate of Inspection and Survey (CIS). 5. System updates 
transaction status to 'Provincially Approved'. 6. System pushes approved 
digital package back to Municipal Assessor and Treasury databases in real time. 
Postconditions: Property transaction is officially approved. Municipal Assessor 
can print the final Tax Declaration, and Treasury can issue real property tax 
bills. 
5.4 Prioritized User Stories 
User Story 1 (High Priority): "As a Municipal Assessor, I want to bundle and 
transmit assessment dossiers online to the Provincial Capitol so that our staff 
eliminates weekly car trips to Pili and avoids 2-to-3-week courier delays." 
User Story 2 (High Priority): "As a Provincial Review Examiner, I want to 
inspect submitted dockets and annotate discrepancies within a structured web 
interface so that incomplete files are caught immediately without physical 
paper damage." 
User Story 3 (High Priority): "As the Provincial Assessor, I want to execute 
legally binding cryptographic approvals and generate verified CIS certificates 
online so that component municipalities receive finalized approvals 
instantaneously." 
User Story 4 (High Priority): "As a Municipal Assessment Clerk, I want to 
monitor live Capitol review milestone statuses online so that I can provide 
instant, transparent progress updates to taxpayers inquiring at the counter." 
User Story 5 (Medium Priority): "As a Municipal Treasurer, I want to receive 
approved assessment records automatically via system synchronization so that 
accurate tax bills and clearances can be generated without manual delays." 
6. Assumptions, Constraints, and Open Issues 
6.1 System Assumptions 
 
 
Broadband Connectivity: Municipal Assessor offices (Pasacao, Canaman) and the 
Provincial Capitol in Pili maintain baseline broadband connections (minimum 10 
Mbps). 
Hardware Readiness: Participating offices possess standard desktop PCs running 
modern web browsers capable of rendering PDF documents and responsive 
dashboards. 
Administrative Willingness: Provincial Assessor leadership in Pili will permit 
designated personnel to participate in controlled pilot trials and evaluation 
surveys. 
Legal Recognition: Electronic approvals, cryptographic hashes, and digital 
stamps produced by the system are recognized as valid administrative acts under 
the Electronic Commerce Act of 2000 (RA 8792). 
 
6.2 System Constraints 
Technical Constraints: Direct read/write access to internal mainframe servers of 
the Provincial Capitol is prohibited by provincial cybersecurity policies. The 
pipeline must operate as an independent, authenticated web portal. 
Policy & Statutory Constraints: Under Section 202 of RA 7160, component 
municipalities cannot grant final assessment approvals. The system must 
strictly preserve the statutory two-tiered hierarchy (Municipal Recommending -> 
Provincial Final Approval). 
Data Privacy Constraints: Must strictly conform to the Data Privacy Act of 2012 
(RA 10173) regarding land titles, taxpayer identification, property valuations, 
and financial figures. 
Budgetary Constraints: Software architecture must utilize open-source frameworks 
(Python FastAPI/Node.js, React, PostgreSQL) to avoid software licensing burdens 
for resource-constrained LGUs. 
Time Constraints: Software development, simulation trials, pilot testing, and 
thesis manuscript defense must be fully accomplished within the 6-month 
capstone period (July to December 2026). 
6.3 Open Issues and Questions 
PNPKI Integration vs. Platform Tokens: Determining whether the Provincial 
Assessor will require integration with the DICT Philippine National Public Key 
Infrastructure (PNPKI) digital certificate authority, or if platform-level 
SHA-256 tokens and secure QR stamps are sufficient for initial deployment. 
Physical Document Retention Policy: Establishing whether provincial examiners 
will require periodic post-audit physical inspections of original notarized 
deeds of conveyance, even when digital dossiers have been approved online.

7.2 Standardized Interview Questions Administered 
Question 1: What are the most frequent real property assessment transactions 
handled daily and monthly in your office? 
Question 2: What specific documentary requirements are mandatory for a Transfer 
of Assessment, and how are non-owner representatives validated? 
Question 3: What is the step-by-step workflow for property appraisal, and how 
are base market values and assessed values calculated? 
Question 4: How are property records stored, indexed, and retrieved? How much 
time is spent retrieving historical records (dating back to 1949)? 
Question 5: How does your office coordinate with the Municipal Treasurer's 
Office, and how are assessment rolls and tax payment records reconciled? 
Question 6: What is the exact process and turnaround time for securing 
endorsements and approvals from the Provincial Capitol in Pili? 
Question 7: What software tools (e.g., eRPT, Excel) are currently deployed, what 
are their main technical limitations, and what features would optimize 
operations?
