Module assigned : Treasurer's Office Tax Collection and Billing Management Module 
 
1. Project overview and stakeholder list 
 
●​ Overview  
○​ Our project group is developing an automated web platform titled as Needs 
Assessment for a Real Property Assessment Tracking and Inter-Office 
Synchronization System: Resolving Disconnected Records and Workflow 
Delays in Municipal Assessor and Treasurer Offices in Pasacao. The main 
goal of our study is to conduct a needs assessment and comparative evaluation 
of local municipal workflows across Canaman, Magarao, San Fernando and 
benchmarking with Pasacao (which uses an existing eRPT setup) to solve 
real-world problems in local government units. Specifically, our project addresses 
the severe administrative frictions, valuation delays, fragmented record-keeping, 
and  the massive operational headache of migrating decades-old legacy records 
dating back to 1949. 
 
Building on this overall group project, my assigned module is the Treasurer's 
Office Tax Collection and Billing Management Module. This module receives 
the Assessment Roll from Module 1, generates Real Property Tax billing 
statements, records payments, tracks delinquent accounts, and issues tax 
clearances. The module addresses three gaps found during field interviews: the 
Treasurer's Office receives Assessment Roll data only through manual 
submission from the Assessor's Office, no digital billing statement is issued to 
taxpayers before the collection window, and there is no automated system to flag 
and track delinquent properties. 
 
●​ Stakeholders and Interest / Concerns 
○​ Municipal Treasurer (Canaman, Pasacao) 
■​ Roles and Interest : Manages real property tax collection. Needs an 
accurate billing ledger, automated computation of discounts and 
penalties, and a delinquency tracking list. 
○​ Treasurer's Office Staff and Collection Clerks 
■​ Roles and Interest: Process walk-in payments and issue tax clearances 
daily. Need fast taxpayer record lookup, pre-computed tax bills, and 
payment receipt generation. 
○​ Real Property Owners and Taxpayers 
1 
■​ Roles and Interest: Pay real property tax annually or in quarterly 
installments. Need a clear billing statement showing the amount due, 
applicable discount or penalty, and payment options. 
○​ IT Developer and Capstone Team 
■​ Designs, builds, and evaluates the system against ISO/IEC 25010. 
 
 
2. Information-gathering methods used and summary of findings 
 
​
To gather these insights, our group used several information-gathering methods, 
including field interviews with municipal personnel in Canaman and Pasacao, analysis of official 
documents and assessment records, comparative observations between manual and eRPT 
workflows, and consultations with our research advisers. 
 
Methods Used 
 
●​ Field Interviews: The team conducted structured interviews with the Assessor's Office 
staff at Canaman and Pasacao. Those interviews revealed details about the workflow 
connection between the Assessor's Office and the Treasurer's Office. A separate 
interview session at the Canaman office included a Treasurer's Office staff member who 
described the collection process. 
●​ Document Analysis:  The team reviewed the discount and penalty schedule as 
described by interviewees. The team also reviewed the Assessment Roll submission 
process as described by the Canaman Assessor. 
●​ Comparative Observation : The team compared the manual collection workflow of 
Canaman (paper ledgers and walk-in counter payments) against the described workflow 
of Pasacao, where the Assessor's Office uses an eRPT system that computes the 
Assessed Value used as the basis for tax billing. 
●​ Professor Consultations: We consulted our project to the professors for guidance on 
our research direction. Their main advice was that our project must focus first on fixing 
local office connections linking the local assessor and treasurer offices so they share 
information smoothly before trying to solve complex provincial Capitol approval delays.  
 
Key Findings 
 
From Canaman (Manual Baseline)  
●​ Office Coordination: The Canaman Assessor confirmed that the Assessor's Office 
provides property records and Assessment Roll data to the Treasurer's Office, noting 
that tax collection is handled entirely by the Treasurer.  
●​ Discounts and Penalties: The office follows the discount and penalty structure under 
Republic Act No. 7160: a 20% discount for advance payment by December of the 
current tax year, a 10% discount for payment within the first quarter, and a 2% per 
month penalty for late payments.  
2 
●​ Back-Year Collections: Taxpayers frequently arrive at the counter with very old 
records to pay uncollected taxes, requiring the Treasurer's Office to manually handle 
back-year billing and accumulated penalty computations.  
●​ Treasury Billing & Payment Process: A Treasurer's Office staff member confirmed 
that the office computes tax bills, collects counter payments, and issues receipts.  
●​ Irregular Payment Patterns: Staff noted that many taxpayers only pay when they 
need a tax clearance for transactions like property transfers or financial assistance, 
making delinquency tracking difficult without a centralized system.  
●​ Assessment Basis & Installments: The treasury uses the Assessed Value from the 
Assessor's Assessment Roll for manual tax computations. Furthermore, payments can 
be made in four equal quarterly installments as prescribed by Republic Act No. 7160, 
Section 250 (due by March 31, June 30, September 30, and December 31).  
 
From Pasacao (Partially Digitized Benchmark)  
●​ System Integration: The Pasacao tax mapper confirmed that their eRPT system 
automatically computes the Assessed Value and passes it to the Treasurer's Office via 
the Assessment Roll. Both offices share data so the treasurer can track tax payments. 
●​ Lack of Online Portals: Similar to manual offices, taxpayers in Pasacao often wait 
until they need a clearance to pay, and there is no digital billing notification or status 
portal available for public use, requiring physical visits to check balances.  
From Professor and Adviser Consultations  
●​ Scope Confirmation: The Capstone Professor confirmed that tax collection and 
payment processing belong strictly to the Treasurer's Office scope. He validated that 
automated billing, statement generation, and delinquency tracking are valid primary 
functions for Module 2. 
●​ Payment Gateways: He noted that online payment via API integration is a future 
enhancement and is not required for this academic year. 
●​ Data Flow: The professor confirmed that the Assessment Roll serves as the direct 
input data source for the billing computation module. 
●​ Systemic Gaps: The Research Adviser confirmed that the workflow disconnection 
between the Assessor's and Treasurer's offices is a systemic problem across all 
visited municipalities, directing the team to treat this data gap as a primary deliverable. 
 
 
3. Functional requirements (prioritized) 
 
MUST HAVE 
●​ This module must receive and import the Assessment Roll from Module 1. The import 
must capture, for each property: the Assessment Roll Number, Property Index 
3 
Number, Owner Name, Property Address, Property Classification, Assessed Value, 
and Tax Declaration effectivity date. This module must not require manual re-entry of 
data that Module 1 already holds. 
●​ This module must compute the annual Real Property Tax due for each property 
automatically. The computation must follow Republic Act No. 7160, Section 233: Basic 
Real Property Tax equals Assessed Value multiplied by the applicable tax rate. For 
provinces, the tax rate must not exceed 1%. The computation must also include the 
Special Education Fund levy of 1% of the Assessed Value. 
●​ This module must compute discounts and penalties automatically. The computation 
must follow Republic Act No. 7160: a 20% discount applies when the taxpayer pays by 
December 31 of the current tax year (Section 251); a 10% discount applies when the 
taxpayer pays within the first quarter of the following year (Section 251); a 2% per 
month penalty applies for each month of delay past the first-quarter deadline, up to a 
maximum of 72% (36 months), as prescribed by Section 255. 
●​ This module must support quarterly installment payment scheduling. The four 
installment due dates must follow Republic Act No. 7160, Section 250: on or before 
March 31, June 30, September 30, and December 31 of the tax year. 
●​ This module must generate a billing statement for each property. The billing statement 
must show: Owner Name, Property Address, Property Classification, Assessed Value, 
Basic Real Property Tax due, Special Education Fund levy, applicable discount or 
penalty, total amount due, and the installment breakdown. 
●​ This module must record each payment transaction. Each record must capture: the 
date of payment, the amount paid, the tax year covered, the installment quarter 
covered, the payment type (full or installment), and the name of the collecting staff 
member. 
●​ This module must issue a payment receipt. The receipt must show: the amount paid, 
the date, the property covered, the tax year, and the receipt number. The receipt must 
be printable. 
●​ This module must allow staff to search taxpayer records by Owner Name, Property 
Index Number, Assessment Roll Number, and Barangay. The module must return 
results in five seconds or less. 
●​ This module must generate a Tax Clearance document for a taxpayer whose account 
has no outstanding balance. The Tax Clearance must state the property covered, the 
tax year verified as paid, and the date of issuance. 
SHOULD HAVE 
●​ This module should flag all delinquent properties automatically. A property is 
delinquent when the first-quarter payment deadline passes without a recorded 
payment. The flag must show the number of months past due and the accumulated 
penalty amount. 
●​ This module should maintain a complete payment history for each property. The 
history must show all payment transactions from the earliest recorded tax year 
available, including back-year payments and adjustments. 
4 
●​ This module should generate a delinquency list report. The report must list all 
properties with outstanding balances, sorted by barangay. Staff must be able to export 
this report in PDF or CSV format. 
●​ This module should compute back-year tax dues automatically. When a taxpayer has 
unpaid taxes from prior years, the module must compute the total due including the 
principal for each year, the accumulated penalty at 2% per month per year, and any 
prior Special Education Fund levy arrears. 
●​ This module should display an alert on the staff dashboard when the December 31 
advance payment discount period is active and when the March 31 first-quarter 
deadline is within 30 days. 
COULD HAVE 
●​ This module could allow taxpayers to check their billing status online without visiting 
the office. The taxpayer must enter the Property Index Number or Assessment Roll 
Number to view the current amount due, payment history, and any outstanding 
balance. 
●​ This module could allow barangay treasurers to record payments for properties within 
their barangay, following the deputization arrangement under Republic Act No. 7160, 
Section 247. Access must be restricted to the barangay the treasurer covers. 
●​ This module could generate a monthly collection summary report. The report must 
show total collections per barangay, per property classification, and per payment type 
for the selected period. 
WON'T HAVE  
●​ This module will not process online payments or connect to payment gateways. The 
Capstone Professor confirmed this is a future API integration. It is outside the scope of 
this academic year. 
●​ This module will not issue electronic fund transfer receipts or connect to any banking 
system. All payments are recorded as walk-in counter transactions. 
●​ This module will not manage non-real-property taxes, fees, or other Treasurer's Office 
collections. This module covers real property tax only. 
●​ This module will not replace the formal physical signing process at the Provincial 
Capitol in Pili. That function is the scope of Module 3.

5. Key use cases or user stories (3–5 high-priority ones) 
 
1.​ Annual Tax Bill Generation from Assessment Roll 
○​ As a Treasurer's Office staff member, I want to import the Assessment Roll from 
the Assessor's Office module and have the module automatically compute each 
property's annual tax bill, so that I can issue billing statements without manually 
re-entering data or computing the amounts by hand  
 
2.​ Walk-in Payment Recording and Receipt Issuance  
○​ As a Treasurer's Office collection clerk, I want to record a taxpayer's payment 
and print a receipt immediately, so that the taxpayer leaves with official proof of 
payment and the record is stored in the module without a separate manual ledger 
entry 
 
3.​ Discount and Penalty Computation 
○​ As a Treasurer's Office staff member, I want the module to automatically show 
the correct discount or penalty based on the current date, so that I can tell the 
taxpayer the accurate amount due without computing it manually at the counter.  
 
4.​ Tax Clearance Issuance 
○​ As a Treasurer's Office staff member, I want to verify that a taxpayer has no 
outstanding real property tax balance and generate a tax clearance document 
instantly, so that the taxpayer can proceed with a transfer of ownership or a 
financial transaction without delay.  
 
5.​ Delinquency List Generation  
○​ As the Municipal Treasurer, I want to generate a list of all delinquent properties 
sorted by barangay, so that I can identify and prioritize collection efforts for 
properties with unpaid real property tax

6. Assumptions, constraints, and open issues 
 
ASSUMPTIONS  
●​ Module 1 will produce an Assessment Roll export in a format that Module 2 can import 
without manual reformatting. The Assessment Roll export format must be confirmed 
between the two module developers before development begins. 
●​ The Municipal Treasurer and at least one collection clerk will participate in system 
evaluation during the pilot phase. 
●​ The team will use mock or masked taxpayer payment data during development and 
testing to comply with Republic Act No. 10173. The team will use actual payment data 
only during the supervised field evaluation phase. 
●​ Internet connectivity at the target Treasurer's Office is adequate for a web-based 
module. The Canaman office confirmed access to a centralized WiFi network. 
●​ Tax rates follow Republic Act No. 7160 and any applicable provincial ordinance. The 
team will confirm the exact tax rate in force for Camarines Sur municipalities before 
encoding the billing computation rules. 
●​ This module covers real property tax collection functions only. Other Treasurer's Office 
collections (business permits, fees, and other local taxes) are outside the scope of this 
module. 
●​ The Tax Clearance document generated by this module requires the Municipal 
Treasurer's physical signature before release during the pilot phase. 
CONSTRAINTS 
●​ Policy: Real property tax rates, discount percentages, and penalty rates follow 
Republic Act No. 7160 (Local Government Code of 1991) and applicable provincial 
ordinances. 
●​ Legal: Maximum interest penalty on delinquent real property tax must not exceed 36 
months (72%) as prescribed by Section 255. 
●​ Legal and Data Privacy: All payment records and taxpayer information are subject to 
Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012) via Role-Based Access Control. 
●​ Time: The academic project timeline runs from July to December 2026 for a working 
prototype deliverable. 
●​ Technical: No external payment gateway integration; all transactions are walk-in 
counter recordings during this phase. 
●​ Regulatory: Current computations follow the existing tax framework rather than the 
2028 targets of Republic Act No. 12001. 
OPEN ISSUES 
●​ Will the Treasurer's Office provide an exported digital dataset of the historical tax 
collection records, or must the development team manually encode the outstanding 
8 
balances and payment ledgers from the physical record books into the system 
database? 
●​ The system must handle payment transactions where the person at the counter is 
acting on behalf of the property owner. Those cases require a Special Power of 
Attorney or a written Authorization Letter. The team must confirm the exact verification 
logic with the Treasurer's Office. 
●​ A Tax Clearance issuance requires verification of all prior-year tax payments and 
arrears. The system currently plans to compute back-year dues based on encoded 
data. Whether any real-time verification of historical physical receipts is feasible within 
the project timeline is not yet decided. 
●​ The team and the Treasurer's Office must agree on how long the office will run both 
the existing manual workflow and the new system at the same time. This comparison 
period provides data for the Paired t-test evaluation. A duration of two weeks is 
proposed. 
●​ The team must confirm the exact Assessment Roll format that the Treasurer's Office 
expects from Module 1. This ensures that the import function successfully processes 
the incoming data schema without compatibility errors.
